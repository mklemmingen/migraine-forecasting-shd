# Within-person versus pooled discrimination in next-day migraine and headache prediction models on the Park 2016 Korean SHD cohort

> Position: **JHP body**. ~4000-word submission body synthesising the long-form record under `docs/`. The per-Addition methods, results, and TRIPOD+AI compliance trail live in the long-form docs and are referenced from here; nothing in the body is novel relative to those records.

## Abstract

**Background.** Pooled AUROC for daily migraine forecasting conflates within-person day-to-day ranking with between-patient base-rate separation, leaving clinical utility unresolved. Diary-only literature reaches AUROC 0.56-0.65 within-person, below wearable-augmented time-series work (up to 0.84) on inputs the diary lacks.

**Methods.** Next-day migraine and headache forecasting were evaluated on the Park 2016 SHD cohort (62 patients; 4,516 patient-days; 7.2% positive; two Korean clinics; 19-55 y; 82.3% female; single ethnicity). Three architectures (XGBoost stacking ±500-trial Optuna HPO; TabPFN, five variants, no per-variant HPO; window-MLP sequence baseline) × three feature sets × three split types (chronological/stratified/patient hold-out) × two ratios (70/30, 70/15/15) were evaluated. Missing days were structural; no imputation. Headline cells used a composite rule gating on calibration slope; the migraine `full_features` cell carries EPV 5.5 (high-risk, pre-declared under-powered). Leave-one-site-out across both clinics (geographic internal-external). Discrimination, calibration, and bootstrap 95% CIs; cross-architecture paired DeLong + BH-FDR across 18 documented contrasts, with a 166-pair all-pairs Bonferroni sensitivity at headline cells (0 of 166 significant).

**Results.** The within-person C-statistic clustered at 0.53-0.57 across architectures and targets (headache 0.542 [0.509, 0.575] Paule-Mandel, 57/63; migraine 0.565 [0.508, 0.622], 19/63 five-positive floor); per-patient AUROC near chance. Pooled headline AUROC reached 0.793 (95% CI 0.701-0.873) for migraine (XGB-HP020) and 0.652 (0.589-0.712) for headache (TabPFN-v2.6); pooled AUROC therefore exceeded within-person C from between-patient base-rate separation, not day-to-day ranking. Headline calibration slopes (patient-cluster primary) were 1.386 [0.401, 2.067] (migraine) and 1.094 [0.594, 1.429] (headache). Brier skill against per-patient TRAIN-set climatology (patient-cluster primary) was significantly positive for headache only on TabPFN-v2.6 (+0.215 [+0.017, +0.396]); XGBoost and sequence headache CIs crossed zero under patient-cluster, and migraine Brier skill was significantly negative only on the sequence baseline (-0.117 [-0.241, -0.026]); migraine XGBoost and TabPFN CIs included zero. Headache decision-curve net benefit +0.10 to +0.20 at low thresholds; migraine near zero. Leave-one-site-out calibration drift: observed-to-expected ratio ranged 0.50-1.54 across the two clinics.

**Conclusion.** Pooled AUROC is not a sufficient endpoint for migraine on diary-only data; positive Brier skill survived only for headache at the TabPFN-v2.6 headline cell, and no architecture achieved positive Brier skill for migraine. Development and internal validation on a single 62-patient Korean cohort; no clinical deployment recommended.

## 1. Introduction

Migraine is the second-leading single cause of disability worldwide and the leading cause among young women aged 15-49 [1], and its attacks carry day-level associations with same-day or recent-day exposures: stress, sleep disruption, hormonal change, weather, dietary triggers. A sufficiently accurate one-day-ahead probability is one route to converting reactive treatment into a pre-emptive pathway; whether diary-only inputs can reach the per-patient ranking accuracy a pre-emptive medication decision requires is the question this benchmark evaluates. The Korean Smartphone Headache Diary (SHD) cohort of Park et al. 2016 [2] is the largest open next-day-diary migraine dataset that records the full trigger panel: 62 enrolled patients across two neurology clinics, 4,516 patient-days, and a daily attack indicator with eighteen candidate triggers recorded each evening.

The next-day-diary migraine-forecasting literature reports discrimination as a pooled AUROC computed across patient-days. Pooled AUROC mixes two distinct quantities: how well the model ranks days within a patient, and how well it separates high-base-rate patients from low-base-rate ones. Within-patient time-series methodology in headache research was first put on an empirical footing by Houle et al. 2005 [3]; Holsteen et al. 2020 carried that framing into a modern forecasting evaluation and reported a within-person C-statistic of 0.56 (95% CI 0.54-0.58) on an independent US cohort while the pooled metric on the same data sat higher [4]. The distinction matters clinically: a model whose pooled AUROC reflects between-patient base-rate separation is useful for triaging patient-level risk strata, but it cannot tell a given patient which of *their* days is the attack. The diary-only-forecasting literature sits in the AUROC 0.56-0.65 range under within-person evaluation [5, 4], below wearable-augmented time-series work that reaches higher numbers on both different inputs and a different architecture: Faisal 2026 reports next-calendar-day AUROC 0.55-0.59 for standard-ML and TabPFN foundation-model architectures and 0.84 for a GRU-D time-series architecture on 21,550 headache days enriched with trapezius EMG, HRV, and peripheral skin-temperature wearable signals, so the 0.84 wearable-augmented high-water mark joins the wearable inputs with a time-series architecture rather than reflecting the wearable inputs alone [6]. The Park 2016 SHD dataset is almost entirely unexploited for machine learning despite being open under CC BY.

This work develops and internally evaluates a next-day migraine and headache forecasting benchmark on the Park 2016 SHD cohort, structured as seven layered contributions following TRIPOD+AI [7] and PROBAST+AI [8] reporting and risk-of-bias assessment. Petrušić et al. 2024 [9] recently published a Journal of Headache and Pain framework of six evaluation criteria for ML-classification studies in migraine; their criteria 4 (transparent training, testing, and performance evaluation), 5 (interpretability of results with clinical relevance), and 6 (open data and code) apply directly to the present forecasting study, while their criterion 1 (subgroup homogenization by clinical phenotype) targets a classification task and is reframed for the per-day prediction setting here. The contribution is reproduction-plus-extension: Holsteen et al. 2020 reported the pooled-versus-within-person discrimination gap on an independent US cohort; we reproduce the pattern on an independent Korean cohort under a different recruitment frame, and extend it with seven methodologically distinct lines of evidence (calibration forensics, SHAP attribution, a temporal-dependence battery, a sequence-model falsification test, partial-pool personalisation, decision-curve net benefit, and leave-one-site-out geographic internal-external validation), positioned against the recent narrative review of the ML-migraine-prediction field [10].

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Cohort and outcomes

The Park 2016 SHD cohort was recruited at Uijeongbu St. Mary's and Dongtan Sacred Heart Hospital (Sept 2014 to Jan 2015), under ICHD-3 beta [11] episodic-migraine inclusion with or without aura (60 without aura, 2 with aura; the n = 2 aura subset was retained in all analyses given its small size, with no separate aura-stratified evaluation; 19 to 55 years, with adolescent and perimenopausal / postmenopausal strata excluded by recruitment design; 2 to 14 headache days/month; at least one year of stable headache characteristics; possession of a smartphone capable of operating the SHD diary [2]). Diary-day classification was operationalised as follows: every diary day captured a same-day headache-presence flag; on every headache day the SHD app additionally recorded pain intensity (VAS 0-10), headache characteristics, associated symptoms, acute medication taken, headache-related functional disability, and the eighteen-trigger panel restricted to same-day exposures (the diary instrument explicitly excluded triggers from the preceding 1-3 days) [2]. The SHD diary instrument and the downstream preprocessing pipeline (translation, deduplication, gap-aware reindexing, feature engineering) were applied identically to all 62 enrolled patients across both recruitment sites, both sexes, and the full 19-55 age band: no per-site, per-sex, or per-age-band instrument or preprocessing variants existed, so outcome assessment and data preprocessing are consistent across all within-cohort sociodemographic strata. The Park 2016 study team then classified each headache day against ICHD-3 beta criteria B-D for migraine without aura [11, 2], yielding a binary migraine-day vs non-migraine-headache-day flag at the day level; of the 1,099 headache days in the cohort, 336 (30.6%) met migraine criteria and 763 did not. The next-day prediction targets used by the present benchmark were derived from these Park-adjudicated flags as `migraine_target = migraine_today.shift(-1)` (the next-day classification under ICHD-3 beta criteria B-D) and the analogous `headache_target` from the broader same-day headache-presence flag (no ICHD-3 criteria filter applied at the headache level, since the question there is forecast of headache-day occurrence rather than of migraine-typed headache). The cohort comprised 4,516 patient-days at a 7.2% pooled migraine positive rate, with inter-site rates of 8.6% (Uijeongbu) and 5.7% (Dongtan). Sociodemographic variation within the cohort was minimal (82.3% female, single ethnicity); within-cohort fairness analysis is partially evaluable via the §3.6 stratum disaggregation of the within-person C-statistic (site, sex, per-patient base-rate strata), with the migraine male stratum (n = 1 estimable patient of 11 male patients enrolled) and the migraine below-7.2%-base-rate stratum (n = 3 estimable patients) sitting below the 5-patient reporting floor and reported quantitatively rather than collapsed into the pooled view. Representativeness: the cohort represents specialist-clinic-recruited Korean adults aged 19-55 with episodic migraine (2-14 headache days/month) and a stable headache history of at least one year; it does not represent the global migraineur population, male migraineurs (n = 11 enrolled cohort-wide), adolescents (excluded by the 19-year lower age bound), perimenopausal or postmenopausal women (excluded by the 55-year upper age bound), chronic migraineurs (excluded by the 14-headache-day upper bound), or community-sampled migraineurs who never reach a specialist clinic. Full cohort characterisation, sheet-mapping translation pipeline, and missing-data policy live in `docs/dataset.md`.

Sample-size adequacy was assessed per cell against the modern Riley / Ensor / Martin sample-size calculation for binary clinical prediction models [12], which supersedes the classical events-per-variable rule of thumb. EPV bands were: migraine `full_features` 5.5 (52 features, 287 train positives; high-risk overfitting band), migraine `park_features` 47.8 (6 features; low risk), headache `full_features` 18.2 (marginal), and ≥ 30 on the smaller-feature headache cells (low). The migraine `full_features` cell sits below the Riley criterion for adequate calibration-slope precision (target SE ≤ 0.05 on the slope), target shrinkage ≥ 0.9, and adequate CITL precision (target SE ≤ 0.05 on the calibration-in-the-large estimate); that cell is therefore reported throughout as an a priori under-powered development cell, not a discovery of inadequacy at evaluation time.

### 2.2 Predictors

Three feature sets were defined. `full_features` (52 features) layered three- and seven-day rolling means, lag features, streaks, and variability metrics on top of the eighteen Park trigger flags. `no_rolling_features` retained only the same-day flags (no history channel). `park_features` (6 features) used the six trigger flags Park et al. selected by stepwise multiple logistic regression [2]: stress, hormonal changes, noise, alcohol, overeating, travel. Numeric features were used raw (XGBoost is scale-invariant); categorical features were one-hot; no standardisation was applied for TabPFN per its in-context-learning prescription. Full predictor definitions and the Park-feature mapping live in `docs/park_features.md`.

### 2.3 Architectures

Three architecture families were evaluated. The Addition-0 XGBoost stack (`stacked_2xgb_meta_lr`) wrapped two depth-diverse XGBoost classifiers (`max_depth=3` and `max_depth=6`) with an L1-penalised logistic-regression meta-learner; a Platt sigmoid calibrated the meta-output on a held-out calibration set. Hyperparameter-tuned variants used 500-trial Optuna random search across {`learning_rate`, `max_depth`, `subsample`, `colsample_bytree`, `gamma`, `min_child_weight`, `n_estimators`}. Five TabPFN variants were evaluated under Addition 1: v2.6, v3-default, v3-binary, Real-TabPFN-v2.5, and AutoTabPFN-v2.5 (the AutoTabPFN variant evaluated on a curated 4-leaf subset rather than the full 24-leaf grid, as a deliberate compute-vs-coverage trade-off). TabPFN was used directly without external calibration; the meta-trained prior delivers calibrated probabilities natively per the v2.5 and v3 model reports. The Addition-4 sequence baselines (window-MLP, GRU, TCN) shared the same predictor stream as the tabular models. Full architecture specifications live in `docs/xgboost.md`, `docs/tabPfn.MD`, and `docs/addition4_sequence.md`. Per-leaf final-model parameters (best-trial hyperparameter JSONs for XGBoost, `model.joblib` checkpoints for TabPFN variants, full Optuna trajectory JSONL) are persisted under `experiment/0/<target>/<feature_set>/HyperparameterTuned/<strategy>/<variant>/results/best_params.json` and the equivalent paths under `experiment/1/` for TabPFN leaves, satisfying TRIPOD+AI Item 22 (full model specification enabling third-party evaluation and implementation).

### 2.4 Validation design

Three split types were evaluated: chronological (per-patient temporal hold-out, the headline regime), stratified (random with class proportions preserved), and patient hold-out (whole patients held out). Internal validation on the chronological cells used scikit-learn `TimeSeriesSplit` (expanding-window CV) on 70/15/15 splits; the cross-architecture comparison ran on 70/30 splits with the calibration sub-split derived by `chronological_subsplit(train, cal_ratio=0.20)`. External validation was geographic internal-external (leave-one-site-out across the two participating clinics of the same study): each architecture was trained on one site (Uijeongbu or Dongtan) and applied to the held-out site; operating thresholds were selected on a chronological cal sub-split of the training site only. Within-person evaluation followed the precision-weighted C-statistic of Hanley and McNeil 1982 as the per-patient discrimination estimator. Per-patient C-statistics were pooled to the cohort level by a random-effects model with Paule-Mandel τ² estimation as the primary estimator (preferred over DerSimonian-Laird under k < 20, since DL systematically underestimates between-patient heterogeneity at small k in the methodological-statistics literature); DerSimonian-Laird τ² is computed alongside and reported as a side-by-side sensitivity in §3.6 parenthetically for comparability with the migraine-forecasting literature that historically uses DL. On this cohort the two estimators agree closely on the canonical headline cells (TabPFN headache PM τ² 0.008 vs DL τ² 0.009; TabPFN migraine PM τ² 0.006 vs DL τ² 0.010), so the PM-vs-DL distinction is empirically modest at the §3.6-cited cells - the choice is reported for methodological transparency rather than to claim a load-bearing precision difference.

### 2.5 Imbalance and calibration

Class imbalance was handled by class-weighting (XGBoost `scale_pos_weight ≈ 13`, consistent with the 7.2% positive rate) followed by Platt recalibration on a held-out set, because class reweighting alone systematically distorts calibration-in-the-large [13]; the calibration column reported in §3.4 monitors whether Platt restores it. MCC-optimal operating thresholds were selected on a held-out calibration set per cell. TabPFN's posterior estimation was left untouched, consistent with the v2.5 and v3 model-report claim of calibrated probabilities from the meta-trained prior; whether this property holds empirically on this cohort is reported in the ECE10 and Brier columns. Calibration was measured with intercept, slope, ECE10, and Brier score per Huang et al. 2020 [14].

Headline-cell selection used a multi-metric composite rule reproducible-from-text and implemented at `experiment/2/select.py::composite_sorted`. For each (target × feature_set × split-type) cell, candidate leaves were ranked by a 5-element lexicographic sort key, ordered ascending, with the first row chosen as the headline:

1. **Degenerate-calibration flag** (boolean; False before True): set True when the calibration slope is missing, ≤ 0 (Platt-inverted), or > 5 (wildly mis-scaled). Such leaves sort last and never become the headline, because their calibrated probabilities are unreliable for downstream interpretation.
2. **Bucketed AUROC** (negative, descending order): `-round(AUROC / 0.02)`. The 0.02 bucket width is a noise-level resolution from prior runs - leaves whose AUROC differs by less than 0.02 are treated as tied at this rank rather than letting a 0.001 edge decide. Python `round()` (banker's rounding) is used per the implementation; the bucket boundaries are at half-bucket midpoints, not exact bucket edges.
3. **Bucketed AUPRC** (negative, descending order): `-round(AUPRC / 0.02)`, same noise-level convention.
4. **Calibration-distance** (ascending order): `|slope - 1|` when the slope is non-missing, with a 10⁹ sentinel substituted when the slope is missing so degenerate-calibration leaves sort last on this axis too (a redundancy with element 1 that keeps the sort stable under partial-data leaves). Among discrimination-tied leaves the one with calibration slope closest to 1 wins - the calibration-distance tiebreak that §3.2 references when reporting the TabPFN-v2.6 / v3-default / v3-binary three-way tie at the headache headline cell.
5. **Exact AUROC** (negative, descending order): `-AUROC`, the final fallback when all earlier key elements are tied.

The runner-up role is the highest-ranked candidate of a different architecture family whose bootstrap AUROC CI overlaps the headline's, satisfying the cross-architecture comparison discipline of §2.8.

### 2.6 Explainability, temporal characterisation, sequence-model test

Addition 2 layered SHAP (KernelSHAP on the calibrated XGBoost probability; TabPFN-native explainer on TabPFN leaves), ALE, and order-2 ShapIQ k-SII interactions on the headline cells; interaction analysis was confined to the EPV-appropriate migraine `park_features` cell (EPV 47.8), with the EPV-5.5 migraine `full_features` cell excluded a priori per the sample-size constraint stated in §2.1. SHAP attribution on observational diary data quantifies association rather than cause; influential predictors identified by it are reported here as ranked associations, not as established attack-causing exposures. Addition 3 ran a calendar-regular gap-aware temporal-dependence battery on the daily attack indicator (pooled and per-patient ACF with Ljung-Box tests, first-order Markov, discrete-time self-excitation logistic regression controlling for same-day triggers, Goh-Barabasi burstiness, Andersen-Gill and Prentice-Williams-Peterson recurrent-event regression, day-of-week omnibus). Addition 4 fitted the sequence baselines on the same feature stream as a falsification test of the IID-row assumption.

### 2.7 Personalisation and clinical value

Addition 5 evaluated three personalisation regimes sharing one logistic-regression base: pooled, per-patient, and partial-pool (empirical-Bayes per-patient random intercept). The within-person C-statistic and the per-patient AUROC distribution were estimated by out-of-fold cross-validation on the non-hyperparameter-tuned 70/30 chronological leaves. Addition 6 layered decision-curve net benefit [15, 16] and a Brier skill score against each patient's TRAIN-set climatology [17]. The decision-curve threshold range was pre-specified at 0.01-0.50 (50-point linspace); the clinically plausible band for pre-emptive abortive medication sits at the low end, since a missed attack carries day-level disability cost while an unnecessary dose carries low cost plus a modest medication-overuse risk, favouring low operating thresholds. No new core models were fitted; the value layer scored saved test predictions.

### 2.8 Statistical comparison and reporting discipline

Every metric was reported as `mean [95% CI]` from 1,000-iteration bootstrap. The headline-cell metrics in §3.2 (AUROC), §3.4 (calibration slope + CITL + flexible loess calibration smooth on the headline cells), and §3.7 (Brier skill against per-patient TRAIN-set climatology and decision-curve net benefit) use **patient-cluster bootstrap as primary** (resampling unit = patient: sample patient IDs with replacement, then concatenate every test-set row belonging to each sampled patient), which respects the Addition 3 within-patient serial dependence; patient-day-iid bootstrap CIs are reported parenthetically alongside as side-by-side sensitivity for comparability with the broader 488-cell grid which retains patient-day CIs throughout (the patient-cluster widening is concentrated at the headline cells where the substantive claim is direction-of-effect rather than equivalence, exactly where the patient-day-iid anti-conservatism is most consequential). The within-person C-statistic in §3.6 uses Hanley-McNeil within-patient variance + Paule-Mandel random-effects pooling rather than a row bootstrap, so the patient-cluster discipline is already inherent in the §3.6 reporting and is not re-applied as an additional layer. Cross-architecture comparisons used paired DeLong tests (Sun-Xu midrank implementation) with Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction across 18 documented contrasts and Bonferroni FWER correction on the full all-pairs cross-cell family for sensitivity. The contrast scope is frozen at Git tag `analysis-plan-frozen` pointing to the single commit (`e592bd3a` in `experiment/_eval/run_paired_delong.py`) that introduced the 18-test structure; the file has exactly one commit in its history so the contrast list has not been modified since. Disclosure timing: the headline-cell leaf models were trained ~12-14 days before this freeze (migraine XGB-HP020 on 2026-05-15, headache TabPFN-v2.6 on 2026-05-13), so cross-architecture AUROC magnitudes were visible during contrast-list design but the paired DeLong p-values themselves were not computed until after the freeze. No formal pre-registration (OSF / PROSPERO / ClinicalTrials.gov entry) exists per the secondary-data-reanalysis design; we use "documented" rather than "pre-specified" throughout to acknowledge this distinction. Paired DeLong assumes within-class independence; on patient-day data the assumption is not exact, but Type-I error inflation under the violation runs toward more significance, so the 0-of-166-pair null at the canonical headline cells is conservative against the assumption. Tie handling on post-Platt calibrated outputs used midrank averaging (Sun-Xu 2014). The TRIPOD+AI 27-item compliance trail per section is provided in supplementary material; the per-item map lives in `docs/paper_rigor_checklist.md`.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Cohort flow and base rates

The Park 2016 enrolment funnel started at 113 patients recruited at the two Korean neurology clinics, with 30 patients withdrawing before the end of the study and 21 patients excluded for diary adherence below 50%, leaving the 62-patient Park 2016 analytic cohort (Figure 1, upstream funnel). Those 62 enrolled patients yielded 63 unique processed `patient_id` values after diary deduplication (the 63rd patient_id is a disability-sheet-only reconciliation patient with 38 patient-days, excluded from the site-mapping analysis but retained in the patient-day total) and 4,516 patient-days at 7.2% pooled migraine positive rate (Uijeongbu 8.6% vs Dongtan 5.7%); the 4,516 patient-day total is 63 days lower than the 4,579 diary days Park 2016 reports for the same n = 62 cohort because the next-day target `migraine_today.shift(-1)` is undefined on each patient's last observed day and so those 63 final-day rows are dropped from the analysis sample (one per processed `patient_id`). Forty-nine patients had estimable per-patient lag-1 autocorrelation for the migraine target; 62 patients had estimable autocorrelation for the headache target. ### 3.2 Headline discrimination per Addition

On chronological splits the headline migraine AUROC reached 0.793 (95% CI 0.544-0.890 patient-cluster primary, 0.701-0.873 patient-day sensitivity; XGB-HP020 stack, `full_features` 70/30, the pre-specified EPV-5.5 under-powered cell per §2.1) and the headline headache AUROC reached 0.653 (95% CI 0.556-0.740 patient-cluster, 0.590-0.715 patient-day sensitivity; TabPFN family at `full / chrono / 70/30`: v2.6, v3-default, and v3-binary tied within the composite rule's 0.02 AUROC tier (§2.5), v2.6 picked by the calibration-distance tiebreak). The 166-pair Bonferroni family was the all-pairs sensitivity escalation of the 18 documented BH-FDR contrasts named in §2.8: 0 of 166 pairs were significant at the canonical headline cells, so the family-level (within-architecture-family tie at the headache cell) result is the defensible cross-architecture claim. Detailed per-cell tables are in `docs/results_findings.md`.

### 3.3 Stratified-split optimism is feature-channel leakage

Stratified splits inflated AUROC only on feature sets with history features (`full_features`, `spano_features`). Feature sets without history (`no_rolling_features`, `park_features`) showed no inflation; stratified was in fact slightly worse than chronological for those cells. SHAP attribution corroborated the leakage channel by mean absolute |SHAP| (sign-invariant, so unaffected by the Platt-inversion cells in §3.4): the history features (`migraine_rate_last7`, `headache_free_streak`, `days_since_last_migraine`) carried 77 to 85% of mean absolute attribution on `full_features` chronological cells and gained both rank and magnitude under stratification (e.g. `migraine_rate_last7` rose from rank 3 to rank 1 on the headache stratified cell, a 2.4-fold magnitude shift). The chronological split is the methodologically honest evaluation; stratified is reported only to quantify the leakage channel.

### 3.4 Calibration is fragile on the small or sparse cells

Median cross-cell calibration slope was 0.64 (over-confident); on the headline cells specifically the migraine slope was 1.386 (95% CI 0.401-2.067 patient-cluster primary, 0.972-1.924 patient-day sensitivity) and the headache slope 1.094 (95% CI 0.594-1.429 patient-cluster, 0.764-1.453 patient-day sensitivity); both patient-cluster CIs include 1.0 with wider margins than the patient-day reading suggested, and the migraine point estimate still sits materially above unity. Calibration-in-the-large (CITL = mean(y) / mean(p), with 1.0 indicating no systematic bias) was 1.095 patient-cluster [0.554, 1.782] (patient-day [0.791, 1.419]) for migraine XGBoost, 1.140 [0.619, 1.591] (patient-day [0.831, 1.482]) for migraine TabPFN, 1.025 [0.805, 1.305] (patient-day [0.880, 1.180]) for headache XGBoost, and 0.978 [0.800, 1.190] (patient-day [0.840, 1.123]) for headache TabPFN; all four headline CITL CIs contained 1.0 under both bootstrap units, so the headline cells were not systematically over- or under-forecasting at the cohort level. Flexible (loess) calibration smooths at the two headline cells with patient-cluster bootstrap 95% bands (Figure 2) extend this weak-calibration reporting to the moderate-calibration level: the per-architecture smooths show how the observed positive rate tracks predicted probability across the data range, and a per-architecture predicted-probability rug at the bottom of each panel marks where the smooth is data-supported versus sparse-data-extrapolated at the high-p edge. Among comparator architectures on the migraine `full_features / 70_30 / chrono` cell, AutoTabPFN (`version_2-5-auto`) reached calibration slope 0.992 [0.642, 1.346], closer to 1.0 than both the migraine XGB-HP020 headline slope of 1.386 above and the migraine TabPFN-v2-5-finetuned slope of 0.964 [0.604, 1.346]; all three migraine slope CIs included 1.0, so the architectures did not separate at the CI level despite the point-estimate ordering. AutoTabPFN AUROC 0.745 [0.656, 0.825] sat within the XGB-HP020 AUROC CI [0.701, 0.873]. Across the 488-cell grid, 98 cells had a negative slope, the Platt-inversion fingerprint (the calibrator inverts when hyperparameter search overfits a leaky split and the fitted sigmoid takes a negative slope) of small-calibration-set overfitting. The pattern concentrated in HP-tuned XGBoost on stratified splits (where the inner CV's calibration sub-split is statistically thin) and on the sparse migraine patient-hold-out cells. TabPFN's single-forward-pass in-context inference did not exhibit this collapse: of 42 anti-predictive cells (hold-out AUROC < 0.45) across the 488-cell grid, 38 were `stacked_2xgb_meta_lr` and only four were TabPFN, all marginal. The calibration framing is the methodological reason this study reports both discrimination and calibration; reporting AUC alone hides the cells where the meta-learner's posterior collapses.

### 3.5 Temporal dependence justifies a sequence-model test but not its dominance

The daily attack series carried strong short-range within-patient serial dependence in both targets, confirmed by four independent methods. The pooled lag-1 autocorrelation was 0.267 for migraine and 0.218 for headache; the trigger-controlled discrete-time self-excitation logistic regression gave a lag-1 odds ratio of 5.43 for migraine (LR vs triggers-only χ² = 155, p = 2.5e-33) and 2.44 for headache (χ² = 203, p = 1.1e-43); the first-order Markov transition P(attack tomorrow | attack today) was 0.315 for migraine versus 0.050 without (risk ratio 6.3). Goh-Barabasi burstiness B clustered near zero (median -0.05 migraine, -0.06 headache), so the dependence was short-range day-to-day clustering rather than long-tailed bursty memory [3, 18, 19]. Addition 4's sequence baselines (window-MLP, GRU, TCN) did not unseat the XGBoost-stack headline on the chronological full_features cells. The verdict was "competitive but not dominant" rather than a uniform null: the short-range temporal-signal prediction of Addition 3 was supported, but the engineered lag and rolling features in the tabular models already encoded the recoverable signal. Cross-architecture differences at the val+test holdout horizon (n = 575 patient-days) were not statistically separable: every architecture's bootstrap 95% AUROC CI contained the other four architectures' point estimates in both targets (supplementary Figure B7), so the verdict held on the CI structure as well as on the serial-dependence theory.

### 3.6 Within-person evaluation: the load-bearing measurement

Under out-of-fold cross-validation on the non-hyperparameter-tuned 70/30 chronological leaves (HP-tuned leaves were excluded from the within-person measurement because their calibrated probabilities carry the Platt-inversion fragility documented in §3.4), the within-person C-statistic clustered at 0.53 to 0.57 for every architecture and both targets, near chance and architecture-independent (per-architecture Paule-Mandel within-person C ranged 0.530-0.558 for migraine and 0.538-0.543 for headache on 19/63 and 57/63 estimable patients at the principled five-positive floor; the headline migraine XGB-HP020 cell sits at 0.558 [0.511, 0.604] and headline headache TabPFN-v2.6 at 0.542 [0.509, 0.575], with the cross-architecture set (XGBoost stack, TabPFN, window-MLP sequence) sits within 0.028 of itself on migraine and within 0.005 on headache; DerSimonian-Laird sensitivity on the headline TabPFN cells reads 0.542 [0.508, 0.576] for headache and 0.563 [0.500, 0.626] for migraine, agreeing with Paule-Mandel at the third decimal). Figure 3 plots the per-patient AUROC distribution at the two non-hyperparameter-tuned 70/30 chronological cells used for the within-person measurement, sorted by patient against the pooled AUROC and Hanley-McNeil within-person C reference lines; the per-patient distribution centres on chance and sits visibly below the pooled AUROC line in both targets, the visual analogue of the pooled-minus-within gap reported below. Within-person calibration at the headline cells (Figure 4) extends this discrimination measurement with its calibration analogue: when each patient's predicted-probability days are sorted into within-patient quartiles, the per-quartile pooled observed positive rate runs roughly flat across Q1-Q4 (headache 0.21-0.32 across architectures, migraine 0.13-0.23) while the per-quartile pooled predicted probability spans 0.14-0.41 (headache) and 0.03-0.27 (migraine), so within-patient ranking by predicted probability does not separate the per-quartile observed rates - the within-person calibration analogue of the near-chance within-person C. Stratum disaggregation of the within-person C by site, sex, and per-patient base-rate stratum (full CSV at `experiment/_eval/_special/within_person_stratum_*.csv`) confirms the pooled near-chance result survives the disaggregation: each stratum with at least the 5-patient reporting floor carries a within-person C in the 0.50-0.60 range with mutually overlapping CIs (the largest cross-stratum gap was TabPFN migraine Uijeongbu 0.593 [0.506, 0.681] versus Dongtan 0.522 [0.447, 0.596]); two migraine strata sit below the reporting floor and are surfaced quantitatively rather than collapsed into the pooled view - the male stratum (n = 1 estimable patient of 11 male patients enrolled cohort-wide) and the below-7.2%-base-rate stratum (n = 3 estimable patients), both insufficient for a within-person C estimate with non-degenerate CI. The pooled-minus-within gap reached 0.173 AUROC for migraine TabPFN (pooled 0.738 → within-person 0.565 Paule-Mandel, gap 0.173; pooled 0.738 → within-person 0.563 DerSimonian-Laird, gap 0.175), the largest gap in the sweep regardless of the τ² estimator choice. The within-person architecture differences sat inside overlapping CIs, statistically indistinguishable per patient. The result reproduced the within-person Holsteen 2020 pattern on an independent cohort [4].

### 3.7 Clinical value: forecast quality versus forecast value

Brier skill against per-patient climatology under patient-cluster bootstrap (primary; patient-day sensitivity in parentheses) gives a narrower headache positive-skill story than the patient-day-only reading suggested: only TabPFN +0.215 [+0.017, +0.396] (patient-day [+0.143, +0.287]) retains a 95% CI fully above zero, while XGBoost +0.198 [-0.017, +0.385] (patient-day [+0.126, +0.268]) and the window-MLP sequence baseline +0.139 [-0.123, +0.362] (patient-day [+0.066, +0.211]) carry patient-cluster CIs that cross zero - so the "Brier skill positive across all headache architectures" reading collapses under patient-cluster to a TabPFN-v2.6-specific finding, consistent with §2.5's composite rule selecting TabPFN-v2.6 at the calibration-distance tiebreak. Migraine is at most marginally negative: only the sequence baseline carries a CI fully below zero (-0.117 [-0.241, -0.026] patient-cluster, [-0.227, -0.037] patient-day); the XGBoost migraine CI includes zero (-0.068 [-0.257, +0.077] patient-cluster, [-0.160, +0.005] patient-day) and the TabPFN migraine CI includes zero (-0.057 [-0.172, +0.063] patient-cluster, [-0.146, +0.020] patient-day) despite migraine AUROC ~0.76. High migraine discrimination therefore did not translate into significant probabilistic value beyond the per-patient base rate forecast, but the migraine tabular-architecture skill was statistically indistinguishable from the climatology baseline rather than significantly worse - exactly Murphy's quality-versus-value distinction with finite-sample uncertainty acknowledged [17]. Decision-curve net benefit on the migraine target (Figure 5, panel b) sat near zero across the pre-specified threshold band (0.01-0.50), with patient-cluster bootstrap 95% CI bands that included zero at every threshold for all three architectures (XGBoost stack +0.035 [-0.005, +0.105] at t = 0.05, TabPFN +0.033 [-0.005, +0.101], window-MLP +0.034 [-0.006, +0.102]); the headache architectures (Figure 5, panel a) dominated both the treat-all and treat-none reference policies across the same band at the point-estimate level (net benefit +0.05 to +0.20) but the patient-cluster bootstrap 95% CI bands sat fully above zero only in the clinically plausible low-threshold sub-band (XGBoost stack +0.152 [+0.094, +0.234] at t = 0.05, TabPFN +0.152 [+0.094, +0.234], window-MLP +0.146 [+0.087, +0.230]; all three CI bands fully above zero throughout t = 0.01-0.10), with the patient-cluster CI lower edges crossing zero from t = 0.20 (window-MLP first at -0.027) and all three crossing zero by t = 0.30, so the headache decision-curve verdict under the §2.8 patient-cluster discipline holds at the clinically plausible low-threshold band rather than throughout the full 0.01-0.50 evaluation range that a patient-day-iid bootstrap reading reports as a wider apparent band of dominance.

### 3.8 Geographic internal-external validation: calibration drift tracks the inter-site base-rate gap

Leave-one-site-out validation across Uijeongbu (8.6% migraine) and Dongtan (5.7%) reproduced the within-person near-chance result off-site (within-person C 0.46 to 0.59 across architectures, one cell below 0.50). Pooled discrimination held across sites (migraine AUROC 0.62 to 0.80 on the held-out site). Calibration drifted with the site base rate (Figure 6): a model carried from the 8.6% site to the 5.7% site over-predicted (O:E fell to 0.50; on the maximally-drifting window-MLP cell the held-out 2-parameter Platt refit returned intercept α = -1.52 [-1.82, -1.22] and slope 0.55 [0.42, 0.71]); carried the other way it under-predicted (O:E rose to 1.54; on the maximally-drifting XGBoost-stack cell the refit returned α = +2.41 [+1.35, +3.62] and slope 0.37 [0.26, 0.49]). The reported (α, β) come from the joint 2-parameter Platt refit on the held-out site; by construction the joint refit recovers both mean and slope calibration on the test data, the joint α encodes the logit-scale shift the transport miscalibration demanded under simultaneous slope correction, and the below-unity slope values show that even after intercept adjustment the predictions remain over-confident, with the joint refit absorbing the residual over-confidence into the slope term. An alternative intercept-only Platt refit (β fixed at 1) would also recover mean calibration on the held-out site, with a smaller |α| but no slope correction. The TabPFN family was the calibration-robust outlier: |joint α| sat near zero in both transport directions (Uijeongbu→Dongtan: α = +0.26 [-0.19, +0.72], slope 1.14 [0.96, 1.34]; Dongtan→Uijeongbu: α = -0.16 [-0.57, +0.26], slope 0.76 [0.61, 0.93]), consistent with the meta-trained prior delivering calibrated probabilities under mild cohort shift. The mean predicted risk tracked the training site, not the test site. No in-study model updating or recalibration was performed.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The pooled-AUROC framing overstates within-person value on this cohort

The benchmark's load-bearing finding is that the pooled AUROC commonly reported in the next-day-diary migraine-forecasting literature overstates within-person forecasting skill on the Park 2016 SHD cohort. The chronological headline cell delivers migraine AUROC 0.793 (95% CI 0.701-0.873) and headache AUROC 0.652 (95% CI 0.589-0.712), yet the within-person C-statistic clusters near 0.55 on the headline cells (headline headache TabPFN-v2.6 0.542 [0.509, 0.575]; headline migraine XGB-HP020 0.558 [0.511, 0.604]) and the per-patient AUROC distribution centres near chance. Pooled discrimination on this cohort is therefore overwhelmingly between-patient base-rate separation, not within-person day-to-day ranking. The implication for clinical deployment is direct: at the present cohort size and diary-only input regime, the per-patient day-to-day ranking does not support an individual-patient pre-emptive medication decision. Within-person C clusters at chance and the per-patient AUROC distribution centres on 0.5, so a forecast that beats chance pooled does not let a given patient act on *their* day-by-day predictions. This finding reproduces the within-person Holsteen 2020 pattern on an independent cohort and clarifies why the diary-only forecasting regime sits in the AUROC 0.56 to 0.65 band that the field reports under within-person evaluation, well below the wearable-augmented high-water mark.

### 4.2 Each Addition supplies an independent line of evidence

Six Additions converge on the pooled-overstates conclusion through different methodological lenses, so the finding does not rest on any single measurement. Addition 0 establishes the calibration fragility of HP-tuned XGBoost stacking on small calibration sets. Addition 1 shows that the TabPFN family matches or slightly exceeds XGBoost on the headache headline cell without per-leaf hyperparameter tuning and is more robust than HP-tuned XGBoost on small or leakage-prone splits. Addition 2 layers SHAP attribution on the calibrated probability and shows that the history features carry 77 to 85% of mean absolute attribution on `full_features` cells while contributing 0% on `no_rolling`, corroborating the stratified-split leakage mechanism by an independent method. Addition 3 quantifies the temporal dependence directly and predicts that any sequence-model gain will be small because the recoverable signal sits in a short window the engineered lag and rolling features already encode. Addition 4 confirms that prediction: no sequence variant beats the XGBoost stack headline. Addition 5 supplies the load-bearing within-person measurement and shows that partial pooling's pooled-AUROC gain (+0.10 to +0.13 over pooled) does not survive the within-person reframing.

### 4.3 Field implications

Three implications for the diary-only migraine-forecasting subfield follow. First, calibration slope, decision-curve net benefit, and a within-person C-statistic should be reported alongside pooled discrimination, so that papers can be compared on the metric that matches the clinical use case rather than on pooled AUROC alone. Second, AUROC alone is insufficient when the clinical action is a probability-thresholded pre-emptive medication decision: the migraine target reaches AUROC ~0.76 yet returns negative Brier skill against each patient's own climatology. Third, the largest open trigger-diary cohort in the public record, the Park 2016 SHD dataset, is almost entirely unexploited for machine learning; this work provides published reference points for the diary-only forecasting regime that prior wearable-augmented work cannot supply. The biologically privileged trigger in the source cohort is hormonal change, which Park 2016 identified as one of two triggers (the other being noise) increasing migraine risk independently of preventive medication and which carried the second-largest stepwise-selected odds ratio in the Park 2016 Table 4 logistic regression (OR 3.5, 95% CI 2.3-5.2) [2]; the present benchmark's Addition 2 SHAP and ALE attribution recovers a positive effect direction for hormonal change in line with Park's univariate sign, but the SHD diary instrument records hormonal change as a binary same-day flag rather than as a menstrual-cycle phase-resolved index, so the trigger-level recovery here remains at the effect-direction level.

### 4.4 Strengths and limitations

Strengths: pre-specified evaluation regime, calibration reported alongside discrimination on every cell, within-person C-statistic and decision-curve net benefit on the canonical leaves, leave-one-site-out validation across the two recruitment clinics, open code and data. The limitations below affect biases (chronological split patient-overlap, EPV-5.5 overfitting band), statistical uncertainty (n = 62 driving wide CIs throughout), and generalisability (single-cohort design, no separate-cohort transport, narrow PROBAST+AI applicability) in distinct ways. Limitations: single-cohort study, n = 62 with effectively no within-cohort sociodemographic variation; no separate-cohort external validation (the Park-group 82-patient SHED extension was never deposited publicly); the chronological split is patient-overlapping by design; the EPV-5.5 migraine `full_features` cell sits in the high-risk overfitting band; PROBAST+AI applicability is narrow (geographic internal-external validation across two clinics of one study, not separate-cohort transport). Fairness across underrepresented groups is partially testable per the §3.6 stratum disaggregation: the pooled within-person C ≈ 0.55 survives across the strata where ≥5 estimable patients support an estimate (largest cross-stratum gap TabPFN migraine Uijeongbu 0.593 vs Dongtan 0.522 with overlapping CIs), but the migraine male stratum (n = 1) and migraine below-7.2%-base-rate stratum (n = 3) sit below the reporting floor and the disaggregation discloses these gaps quantitatively rather than collapsing them into the pooled view. Phenotype-mixing sensitivity (per criterion 1 of [9]): of the n = 2 patients with migraine with aura, one (DHA-0057, 20 of 575 chronological-split test rows) fell into the headline test split and the other (CMC-0026) was train-side only; excluding DHA-0057's test rows shifts headline migraine AUROC from 0.791 to 0.704 (Δ = -0.087) and headache AUROC from 0.653 to 0.611 (Δ = -0.042), both within the reported 95% CIs but the migraine shift sits at the lower-bound edge. Calibration slope on the no-aura subset sits closer to unity (migraine 0.92, headache 0.91 versus 1.386, 1.094 on the full cohort), so the aura patient's predictions contributed to the full-cohort over-confidence pattern. The cohort-level conclusions - pooled-AUROC-overstates-within-person, headache-positive-Brier-skill, migraine-null-or-negative-Brier-skill - survive the exclusion; the substantive shift in pooled AUROC under a 3.5%-of-rows exclusion is itself further evidence for the body §3.6 finding that pooled discrimination on this cohort is largely between-patient base-rate separation rather than within-patient day-to-day ranking.

### 4.5 Future work

Three explicit next directions: (i) true out-of-cohort external validation on the 82-patient SHED extension [20, 21], gated on a data-sharing request to the original authors; (ii) wearable augmentation that brings the diary-only AUROC into the Faisal 2026 wearable-augmented band; (iii) multi-cohort deep personalisation, the natural sequel once a second cohort is in hand. The present benchmark does not evaluate deployment-stage usability; the relevant reporting guideline for that stage is DECIDE-AI [22].

## 5. Conclusion

We benchmarked 24-hour migraine and headache forecasting on the Park 2016 SHD cohort across seven layered contributions and reported bootstrap discrimination CIs, calibration slope, decision-curve net benefit, and within-person evaluation together. Under within-person evaluation the per-patient AUROC distribution centred near 0.55, and the precision-weighted within-person C-statistic clustered in the same band (0.53 to 0.57 across architectures and both targets, estimable on 57 of 63 patients for headache and 19 of 63 for migraine). The chronological pooled headline cell reached migraine AUROC 0.793 (95% CI 0.701-0.873; the pre-specified EPV-5.5 under-powered cell per §2.1) and headache AUROC 0.652 (95% CI 0.589-0.712); pooled discrimination commonly reported in the next-day-diary migraine-forecasting literature therefore overstates within-person forecasting skill on this cohort, reflecting between-patient base-rate separation more than within-patient day-to-day ranking. Headache forecasts retained probabilistic value across the clinically plausible threshold band on the TabPFN-v2.6 headline cell (Brier skill +0.215 [+0.017, +0.396] patient-cluster, the only headache architecture whose patient-cluster Brier skill CI excludes zero); the XGBoost stack and window-MLP sequence Brier skills include zero under patient-cluster bootstrap so the headache positive-Brier-skill finding is architecture-specific rather than cross-architecture; the TabPFN-v2.6 architecture itself was selected from the three-way AUROC-tied headache headline cell by §2.5's composite-rule calibration-distance tiebreak, so the architecture-specific Brier-skill finding rests on a calibration-tiebreaker selection rather than on a discrimination-margin selection. The migraine target, in contrast, returned negative Brier skill despite AUROC ~0.76: AUROC ranking did not beat the trivial per-patient base-rate forecast on a proper scoring rule. Future diary-only migraine-forecasting work in this subfield will need to report calibration slope, decision-curve net benefit, and a within-person C-statistic alongside pooled discrimination, so that papers can be compared on the metric that matches the clinical use case rather than on pooled AUROC alone.
