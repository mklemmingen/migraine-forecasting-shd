# Within-person vs pooled discrimination, next-day migraine-headache prediction: Park 2016 Korean SHD

> Position: **JHP body**. ~4000-word submission body synthesising the long-form record under `docs/`. The per-Addition methods, results, and TRIPOD+AI compliance trail live in the long-form docs and are referenced from here; nothing in the body is novel relative to those records.

## Abstract

**Objective.** To develop and internally validate a multivariable next-day forecasting benchmark for episodic migraine and headache on a publicly available Korean smartphone diary cohort, contrasting pooled with within-person discrimination as the load-bearing endpoint.

**Background.** Pooled AUROC for daily migraine forecasting conflates within-person day-to-day ranking with between-patient base-rate separation, leaving clinical utility unresolved. The diary-only literature reaches AUROC 0.56-0.65 within-person, below the wearable-augmented high-water mark (up to 0.84) on inputs the diary lacks.

**Methods.** Next-day migraine and headache forecasting were developed and internally evaluated on the Park 2016 SHD cohort (62 patients; 4,516 patient-days; 7.2% positive; two Korean clinics; 19-55 y; 82.3% female; single ethnicity). Three architectures (XGBoost stack, TabPFN six variants, three sequence baselines) × three feature sets × three split types × two ratios were evaluated. Predictors comprised eighteen Park 2016 same-day trigger flags plus engineered history features. Missing days were treated as structural, with no imputation. Headline cells were selected by a composite rule gating on calibration slope; the migraine `full_features` cell carries EPV 5.5 and is pre-declared under-powered. A leave-one-site-out check provided within-study cross-clinic robustness (not cross-cohort external transport). Bootstrap 95% CIs used patient-cluster resampling. Cross-architecture comparisons used paired DeLong tests with Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction across 18 documented contrasts. Not registered.

**Results.** The within-person C-statistic clustered at 0.53-0.57 across architectures and both targets (headache 0.542 [0.509, 0.575]; migraine 0.558 [0.511, 0.604]); per-patient AUROC centred near chance. Pooled AUROC exceeded within-person C by 0.23 (migraine, on the pre-declared under-powered EPV 5.5 cell whose patient-cluster calibration-slope CI exceeds the Riley/Martin precision guide by approximately 8×) and 0.11 (headache, on the EPV 18.2 cell exceeding the guide by approximately 4.2×), reaching 0.793 (95% CI 0.544-0.890) and 0.653 (0.556-0.740) respectively on the chronological split, which is patient-overlapping by design and tests within-patient temporal generalisation rather than held-out-patient transport; the pooled migraine CI lower bound (0.544) sits barely above chance, so pooled discrimination reflected between-patient base-rate separation rather than within-person day-to-day ranking. Under conservative Bonferroni FWER correction across 166 architecture pairs at the canonical chronological headline cells, 0 contrasts achieved significance; under less-conservative BH-FDR correction across 18 documented contrasts, 1 survived (q ≤ 0.05) at a non-canonical small-cohort patient-hold-out cell, reported as exploratory under the §2.8 visibility caveat. Brier skill against per-patient TRAIN-set climatology was positive for headache only on the TabPFN-v2.6 headline cell (+0.215 [+0.017, +0.396]), where v2.6 was selected by the §2.5 composite-rule calibration-distance tiebreak from a three-way TabPFN AUROC tie (v2.6, v3-default, v3-binary), so the positive Brier-skill result is not independent architecture-level evidence; migraine Brier skill CIs included or fell below zero across architectures. Headache decision-curve net benefit was positive at clinically plausible low thresholds; migraine net benefit sat near zero. Leave-one-site-out calibration drift was observed across the two clinics.

**Conclusion.** Pooled AUROC is not a sufficient endpoint for migraine on diary-only data; positive Brier skill survived only for headache at the TabPFN-v2.6 headline cell, and no architecture achieved positive Brier skill for migraine. Development and internal validation on a single 62-patient Korean cohort; we do not recommend clinical deployment.

**Keywords.** Migraine; Headache; Forecasting; Machine learning; Calibration; Mobile applications; Mobile health; Models, Statistical; Within-person evaluation; Internal-external validation

## Introduction

Migraine remains the second-leading single cause of global disability and the leading cause among young women aged 15-49 [1]; that burden falls disproportionately in the productive-age window, where a one-day-ahead probability could convert reactive treatment into a pre-emptive pathway. Attacks carry day-level associations with same-day or recent-day exposures: stress, sleep disruption, hormonal change, weather, dietary triggers. These exposures are precisely the channels a daily symptom diary records. Whether such diary-only inputs can reach the per-patient ranking accuracy a pre-emptive medication decision requires is the question this benchmark evaluates. The Korean Smartphone Headache Diary (SHD) cohort of Park et al. 2016 [2] is the largest open next-day-diary migraine dataset that records the full trigger panel: 62 enrolled patients across two neurology clinics, 4,516 patient-days, and a daily attack indicator with eighteen candidate triggers recorded each evening.

The next-day-diary migraine-forecasting literature reports discrimination as a pooled AUROC computed across patient-days. Pooled AUROC mixes two distinct quantities: how well the model ranks days within a patient, and how well it separates high-base-rate patients from low-base-rate ones. Within-patient time-series methodology in headache research was first put on an empirical footing by Houle et al. 2005 [3]; Holsteen et al. 2020 carried that framing into a modern forecasting evaluation and reported a within-person C-statistic of 0.56 (95% CI 0.54-0.58) on an independent US cohort while the pooled metric on the same data sat higher [4]. The distinction matters clinically: a model whose pooled AUROC reflects between-patient base-rate separation is useful for triaging patient-level risk strata, but it cannot tell a given patient which of *their* days is the attack. Houle et al. 2017 [5] and Holsteen et al. 2020 [4] both place diary-only within-person discrimination in the AUROC 0.56-0.65 range, below wearable-augmented time-series work that reaches higher numbers on both different inputs and a different architecture: Faisal 2026 reports next-calendar-day AUROC 0.55-0.59 for standard-ML and TabPFN foundation-model architectures and 0.84 for a GRU-D time-series architecture on 21,550 headache days enriched with trapezius EMG, HRV, and peripheral skin-temperature wearable signals, so the 0.84 wearable-augmented high-water mark joins the wearable inputs with a time-series architecture rather than reflecting the wearable inputs alone [6]. At a cohort scale closer to the present benchmark (18 episodic-migraine patients on a wearable-augmented diary), Stubberud et al. 2023 reported held-out AUC 0.62 [23], confirming that the high-water mark is reached only at cohort scales an order of magnitude above the diary-only single-clinic regime. The Park 2016 SHD dataset is almost entirely unexploited for machine learning despite being open under CC BY: the most recent Korean systematic review of AI in headache disorders does not identify any ML study using this cohort [33].

This work develops and internally evaluates a next-day migraine and headache forecasting benchmark on the Park 2016 SHD cohort. The load-bearing finding is that on this cohort the within-person C-statistic clusters near chance (0.53-0.57) while pooled AUROC reaches 0.79 (migraine) / 0.65 (headache), so per-day predictions cannot guide individual-patient decisions despite the apparent pooled discrimination. Three top-line contributions follow TRIPOD+AI [7] and PROBAST+AI [8] reporting and risk-of-bias assessment: (1) the personalisation-evaluation layer measuring the within-person C-statistic and the pooled-vs-within gap (0.23 migraine, 0.11 headache); (2) the clinical-value layer reporting Brier skill against per-patient climatology and decision-curve net benefit; (3) a falsification battery (temporal-dependence + sequence-model test) showing the within-person near-chance ceiling is regime-level rather than architecture-level. The XGBoost stack baseline, TabPFN family evaluation, SHAP/ALE attribution, and leave-one-site-out within-study cross-clinic robustness check are supporting layers; their detailed roles are in §2 and §3. Petrušić et al. 2024 [9] recently published a Journal of Headache and Pain framework of six evaluation criteria for ML-classification studies in migraine; their criteria 4 (transparent training, testing, and performance evaluation), 5 (interpretability of results with clinical relevance), and 6 (open data and code) apply directly to the present forecasting study, while their criterion 1 (subgroup homogenization by clinical phenotype) targets a classification task and is reframed for the per-day prediction setting here. The contribution is reproduction-plus-extension: Holsteen et al. 2020 reported the pooled-versus-within-person discrimination gap on an independent US cohort; we reproduce the pattern on an independent Korean cohort under a different recruitment frame, positioned against recent narrative reviews of the ML-migraine-prediction field [10, 34]; Stubberud et al. 2024 explicitly flags the between-person versus within-person discrimination distinction as an open methodological direction for the field.

**Reading guide and plain-terms glossary.** The within-person C-statistic is the load-bearing endpoint of this paper: it summarises, per patient, the probability that the forecast correctly orders the patient's attack days above their non-attack days; ~0.55 here means roughly coin-flip per-patient day-ranking, against a ~0.70 threshold for actionable use. Brier skill compares the forecast against the patient's own historical attack rate; positive Brier skill means the forecast adds information beyond climatology. Calibration slope ≈ 1 means predicted probabilities match observed rates. Four other terms appear early in Methods. **Platt scaling** retrains a small logistic-regression layer on top of the raw model so the predicted probabilities match observed event rates on a held-out calibration sample. **BH-FDR** (Benjamini-Hochberg false discovery rate) is a less-conservative correction for multiple comparisons than Bonferroni; it controls the expected proportion of false positives among the rejected nulls. **Paule-Mandel τ²** is a random-effects variance estimator preferred over DerSimonian-Laird when the number of pooled units is small (k < 20). **EPV** (events per variable) is a sample-size adequacy heuristic; an EPV near 20 is the conventional threshold for adequate model precision.

## Methods

### Cohort and outcomes

The Park 2016 SHD cohort was recruited at Uijeongbu St. Mary's and Dongtan Sacred Heart Hospital between September 2014 and January 2015. Inclusion followed ICHD-3 beta criteria for episodic migraine with or without aura [11], yielding 60 patients without aura and 2 with aura; the n = 2 aura subset was retained in all analyses given its small size, with no separate aura-stratified evaluation. Eligibility required age 19 to 55 (adolescent and perimenopausal / postmenopausal strata excluded by recruitment design), 2 to 14 headache days per month, at least one year of stable headache characteristics, and possession of a smartphone capable of operating the SHD diary [2].

Diary-day classification followed a fixed protocol. Every diary day captured a same-day headache-presence flag; on every headache day the SHD app additionally recorded pain intensity (VAS 0-10), headache characteristics, associated symptoms, acute medication taken, headache-related functional disability, and the eighteen-trigger panel restricted to same-day exposures; the diary instrument explicitly excluded triggers from the preceding 1-3 days [2]. The SHD diary instrument and the downstream preprocessing pipeline (translation, deduplication, gap-aware reindexing, feature engineering) were applied identically to all 62 enrolled patients across both recruitment sites, both sexes, and the full 19-55 age band, so outcome assessment and data preprocessing are consistent across all within-cohort sociodemographic strata. The Park 2016 study team then classified each headache day against ICHD-3 beta criteria B-D for migraine without aura [11, 2]. Criteria B-D were operationally unchanged between the 2013 ICHD-3 beta version and the 2018 finalised ICHD-3 [11], so the day-level adjudication conforms to the current ICHD-3 standard. ICHD-3 1.1 criterion A (≥ 5 lifetime attacks fulfilling B-D) and criterion E (not better accounted for by another ICHD-3 diagnosis) are inherently patient-level rather than day-level, so this day-level adjudication is a phenotype-feature contrast within an already-diagnosed migraineur cohort rather than a fresh per-day ICHD-3 diagnosis. Of the 1,099 headache days in the cohort, 336 (30.6%) met migraine criteria and 763 did not. The next-day prediction targets were derived from these Park-adjudicated flags as `migraine_target = migraine_today.shift(-1)` and the analogous `headache_target` from the broader same-day headache-presence flag. Holsteen et al. 2020 [4] excluded post-migraine Recovery days from their at-risk pool and explicitly stated that "predicting only new-onset migraines is intuitively more relevant and should be the standard for future migraine prediction efforts" (Holsteen 2020 p.2369). The present target retains continuation days (any next-day migraine, not new-onset only), which makes the prediction task statistically easier through the lag-1 channel that §3.5 quantifies directly (lag-1 OR 5.43 for migraine; first-order Markov P(attack tomorrow | attack today) = 0.315 vs 0.050 without). The construct difference is therefore real and the cross-cohort comparison to Holsteen 2020 is reported throughout as directionally consistent rather than as a strict reproduction (§3.6 + §4.1).

The cohort comprised 4,516 patient-days at a 7.2% pooled migraine positive rate, with inter-site rates of 8.6% (Uijeongbu) and 5.7% (Dongtan). Sex distribution was 82.3% female (n = 51 of 62) and ethnicity was single-strata Korean, which constrains generalisability to the underrepresented strata documented below. Within-cohort fairness analysis is partially evaluable via the §3.6 stratum disaggregation of the within-person C-statistic by site, sex, and per-patient base-rate stratum; the migraine male stratum (n = 1 estimable patient of 11 male patients enrolled) and the migraine below-7.2%-base-rate stratum (n = 3 estimable patients) sit below the 5-patient reporting floor and are reported quantitatively rather than collapsed into the pooled view. The cohort represents specialist-clinic-recruited Korean adults aged 19-55 with episodic migraine and a stable headache history of at least one year; it does not represent the global migraineur population, male migraineurs (n = 11 enrolled cohort-wide), adolescents, perimenopausal or postmenopausal women, chronic migraineurs (excluded by the 14-headache-day upper bound), or community-sampled migraineurs who never reach a specialist clinic. Full cohort characterisation, sheet-mapping translation pipeline, and missing-data policy live in `docs/dataset.md`.

Sample-size adequacy was assessed per cell on two axes: events-per-variable (EPV) bands as the substantive flag, and calibration-slope-CI width as a Riley / Ensor / Martin precision check [12]; the closed-form Cox-Snell R² calculation was not run, the EPV ratio at 7.2% migraine prevalence and 52 candidate parameters serving as the substantive sample-size signal. EPV bands were: migraine `full_features` 5.5 (52 features, 287 train positives; high-risk overfitting band), migraine `park_features` 47.8 (6 features; low risk), headache `full_features` 18.2 (marginal), and ≥ 30 on the smaller-feature headache cells (low). The migraine `full_features` cell sits in the high-risk overfitting band and is reported throughout as an a priori under-powered development cell, not a discovery of inadequacy at evaluation time. On the Riley-school slope-precision axis, both headline cells fail the target slope-CI width < 0.2 (equivalent to slope SE ≤ 0.05): the headache `full_features` cell's patient-cluster calibration-slope 95% CI [0.594, 1.429] has width 0.835, exceeding the target by approximately 4.2×; the migraine `full_features` cell's patient-cluster slope CI width 1.666 exceeds the target by approximately 8.3×. The Riley precision standard is therefore not met on either headline cell. The closed-form Cox-Snell R² calculation was replaced by the empirical calibration-slope-CI check reported above, following the same EPV-band-with-calibration-overfitting-diagnostic approach Holsteen et al. 2020 [4] adopted on their n = 178 cohort (where they reported calibration-in-the-large and a calibration slope of 0.73 [0.57, 0.90] as their overfitting diagnostic without running the closed-form a-priori derivation).

### Predictors

Three feature sets were defined. `full_features` (52 features) layered three- and seven-day rolling means, lag features, streaks, and variability metrics on top of the eighteen Park trigger flags. `no_rolling_features` retained only the same-day flags (no history channel). `park_features` (6 features) used the six trigger flags Park et al. selected by stepwise multiple logistic regression [2]: stress, hormonal changes, noise, alcohol, overeating, travel. Numeric features were used raw (XGBoost is scale-invariant); categorical features were one-hot; no standardisation was applied for TabPFN per its in-context-learning prescription. Full predictor definitions and the Park-feature mapping live in `docs/park_features.md`.

### Architectures

Three architecture families were evaluated.

**XGBoost stack.** The calibrated XGBoost stack (`stacked_2xgb_meta_lr`) wrapped two depth-diverse XGBoost classifiers (`max_depth=3` and `max_depth=6`) with an L1-penalised logistic-regression meta-learner; a Platt sigmoid calibrated the meta-output on a held-out calibration set. Hyperparameter-tuned variants used 500-trial Optuna random search across {`learning_rate`, `max_depth`, `subsample`, `colsample_bytree`, `gamma`, `min_child_weight`, `n_estimators`}.

**TabPFN family.** Six TabPFN variants were evaluated [27]: v2.6, v3-default, v3-binary, Real-TabPFN-v2.5, AutoTabPFN-v2.5, and fine-tuned TabPFN-v2.5 (the AutoTabPFN variant evaluated on a curated 4-leaf subset rather than the full 24-leaf grid, as a deliberate compute-vs-coverage trade-off). TabPFN was used directly without external calibration; the meta-trained prior delivers calibrated probabilities natively per the v2.5 and v3 model reports [28, 29]. TabPFN's published evaluations address IID-row tabular data with up to approximately 10,000 samples and up to a few hundred features [27, 28, 29]; the present regime adds within-patient clustering across 62 patients and a 7.2% pooled positive prevalence, conditions not separately addressed by the v2.5 / v3 evaluation envelope. The native-calibration claim therefore applies under documented evaluation conditions, not at this regime; calibration on this cohort is reported empirically at §3.4 alongside the other architectures rather than asserted from the meta-trained prior. The dataset's 4,516 patient-days sit below the 10,000-sample envelope ceiling, so chunking was not required.

**Sequence baselines.** The sequence baselines (window-MLP, GRU, 1D-CNN) consumed the same engineered predictor stream as the tabular models plus a per-step gap channel encoding the normalised time-interval since the previous retained day (n_features = 53 on full_features), following the GRU-D treatment of irregularly sampled clinical series with missing-pattern and time-since-last-observation channels [24].

A schematic of the full benchmark pipeline (Diary → Features → Splits×Ratios → Architectures → Evaluation) with the explainability, temporal-dependence, personalisation, and clinical-value layers is provided as Supplementary Figure S1. Full architecture specifications live in `docs/xgboost.md`, `docs/tabPfn.MD`, and `docs/addition4_sequence.md`. Per-leaf final-model parameters are persisted as calibrated bundles for both XGBoost and TabPFN leaves; the XGBoost HP-tuned branch additionally writes the full Optuna search history, from which the selected per-trial hyperparameters are recoverable via the variant-selection rule. Full artefact paths and the variant-selection rule are documented in the released code repository. Together these artefacts satisfy TRIPOD+AI Item 22 (full model specification enabling third-party evaluation and implementation).

### Validation design

We evaluated three split types: chronological, stratified, and patient hold-out. The chronological split applied a global-date temporal hold-out per row across the cohort, so every patient contributed rows to train, cal, and test through different date windows; the patient-overlap consequence is disclosed at §3.1 and §4.4. This was the headline regime. The stratified split was random with class proportions preserved. The patient hold-out split held out whole patients.

Each split type addresses a distinct validation question. Chronological-patient-overlapping tests within-patient temporal generalisation: the deployment scenario of forecasting today for a returning user whose history the model already has. Stratified quantifies the leakage channel that random partitioning opens against the temporal-honest regime. Patient hold-out tests held-out-patient generalisation: the deployment scenario of applying the model to a new patient with no prior history.

Internal validation on the chronological cells used scikit-learn `TimeSeriesSplit` (expanding-window CV) on 70/15/15 splits. The cross-architecture comparison ran on 70/30 splits, with the calibration sub-split derived by `chronological_subsplit(train, cal_ratio=0.20)`. The leave-one-site-out check is within-study cross-clinic robustness across the two participating clinics of the same study, not cross-cohort external transport: each architecture was trained on one site (Uijeongbu or Dongtan) and applied to the held-out site. Operating thresholds were selected on a chronological cal sub-split of the training site only.

Within-person evaluation followed the precision-weighted C-statistic of Hanley and McNeil 1982 [30]. For each patient, the C-statistic summarises the proportion of discordant day-pairs in which the attack day carried the higher predicted risk. Holsteen et al. 2020 adopted this definition for the subfield [4]. Per-patient C-statistics were pooled by a random-effects model with Paule-Mandel τ² as the primary estimator. We preferred Paule-Mandel over DerSimonian-Laird because DL systematically underestimates between-patient heterogeneity at k < 20. DerSimonian-Laird τ² is computed alongside as a side-by-side sensitivity in §3.6, for comparability with the migraine-forecasting literature that historically uses DL.

On this cohort the two estimators agree closely on the canonical headline cells (TabPFN headache PM τ² 0.008 vs DL τ² 0.009; TabPFN migraine PM τ² 0.006 vs DL τ² 0.010). The PM-vs-DL distinction is empirically modest at the §3.6-cited cells. The choice is reported for methodological transparency rather than to claim a load-bearing precision difference.

### Imbalance and calibration

Class imbalance was handled by class-weighting (XGBoost `scale_pos_weight ≈ 13`, consistent with the 7.2% positive rate) followed by Platt recalibration on a held-out set, because class reweighting alone systematically distorts calibration-in-the-large [13]; the calibration column reported in §3.4 monitors whether Platt restores it. MCC-optimal operating thresholds were selected on a held-out calibration set per cell; at evaluate time both the Platt calibrator and the MCC-optimal threshold were fit on the full chronological calibration sub-split of the training site, with the locked test set untouched in both fitting steps. TabPFN's posterior estimation was left untouched, consistent with the v2.5 and v3 model-report claim that the meta-trained prior delivers strong calibration at default scores (uncalibrated, no temperature-scaling, no threshold-tuning) [28, 29]; whether this property holds empirically on this cohort is reported in the ECE10 and Brier columns. Calibration was measured with intercept, slope, ECE10, and Brier score per Huang et al. 2020 [14].

Headline-cell selection used a multi-metric composite rule with the following lexicographic priority:

1. **Validity gate**: leaves with degenerate calibration (missing slope, slope ≤ 0, or slope > 5) sort last and never become headlines.
2. **Discrimination bucket**: leaves are grouped into 0.02 AUROC tiers, a noise-level bucket width calibrated from prior-run bootstrap variance.
3. **Calibration-distance tiebreak within a tier**: among leaves tied within the same 0.02 AUROC bucket, the candidate with calibration slope closest to 1 wins. This is the tiebreak §3.2 references at the TabPFN-v2.6 / v3-default / v3-binary three-way tie at the headache headline cell.

The full 5-element lexicographic sort specification, the AUPRC and exact-AUROC fallbacks, and the bucket-width derivation are in Supplementary Methods. The runner-up role is the highest-ranked candidate of a different architecture family whose bootstrap AUROC CI overlaps the headline's, satisfying the cross-architecture comparison discipline of §2.8.

### Explainability, temporal characterisation, and clinical value

The explainability layer combined SHAP (KernelSHAP on the calibrated XGBoost probability; TabPFN-native explainer on TabPFN leaves), ALE, and order-2 ShapIQ k-SII interactions on the headline cells; interaction analysis was confined to the EPV-appropriate migraine `park_features` cell, with the migraine `full_features` cell excluded a priori per the sample-size constraint stated in §2.1. SHAP attribution on observational diary data quantifies association rather than cause; influential predictors identified by it are reported here as ranked associations, not as established attack-causing exposures. The temporal-dependence battery ran a calendar-regular gap-aware analysis of the daily attack indicator (pooled and per-patient ACF with Ljung-Box tests, first-order Markov, discrete-time self-excitation logistic regression controlling for same-day triggers, Goh-Barabasi burstiness, Andersen-Gill and Prentice-Williams-Peterson recurrent-event regression, day-of-week omnibus). The sequence-model falsification test fitted window-MLP, GRU, and 1D-CNN baselines on the same feature stream to test the IID-row assumption.

The personalisation evaluation compared three regimes sharing one logistic-regression base: pooled, per-patient, and partial-pool (empirical-Bayes per-patient random intercept). The within-person C-statistic and the per-patient AUROC distribution were estimated by out-of-fold cross-validation on the non-hyperparameter-tuned 70/30 chronological leaves. The clinical-value layer reported decision-curve net benefit [15, 16] and a Brier skill score against each patient's TRAIN-set climatology [17]. The decision-curve threshold range was documented at 0.01-0.50 (50-point linspace); the clinically plausible band for pre-emptive abortive medication sits at the low end, since a missed attack carries day-level disability cost while an unnecessary dose carries the iatrogenic risk of medication-overuse headache (ICHD-3 8.2 thresholds: triptan / opioid use on > 10 days/month, simple-analgesic / NSAID use on > 15 days/month, or combination-medication use on > 10 days/month, each sustained for > 3 months), constraining how often any low-threshold-triggered pre-emptive policy can fire without flipping into the MOH regime; the threshold band still favours low operating thresholds but the dose-frequency ceiling is an implicit upper limit on the actionable policy frequency rather than an unconstrained low-threshold optimum. No new core models were fitted; the value layer scored saved test predictions.

### Statistical comparison and reporting discipline

Every metric was reported as `mean [95% CI]` from bootstrap resampling. Iteration counts were 1,000 for the headline-cell AUROC, calibration slope, CITL, and Brier-skill metrics and for the 488-cell-grid sensitivity; 500 for the decision-curve net benefit (Figure 7); and 300 for the flexible loess calibration smooth (Figure 3).

The headline-cell metrics in §3.2, §3.4, and §3.7 use **patient-cluster bootstrap as primary**. The resampling unit is the patient: patient IDs are sampled with replacement, and every test-set row belonging to each sampled patient is concatenated. This respects the within-patient serial dependence quantified in §3.5. Patient-day-iid bootstrap CIs are reported parenthetically alongside as a side-by-side sensitivity for comparability with the broader 488-cell grid, which retains patient-day CIs throughout. The patient-cluster widening is concentrated at the headline cells, where the substantive claim is direction-of-effect rather than equivalence and where patient-day-iid anti-conservatism is most consequential. The within-person C-statistic in §3.6 uses Hanley-McNeil within-patient variance with Paule-Mandel random-effects pooling rather than a row bootstrap, so the patient-cluster discipline is inherent in §3.6 reporting and is not re-applied as an additional layer.

Cross-architecture comparisons used paired DeLong tests with Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction across 18 documented contrasts. The 18 contrasts were exhaustive within five categories: cross-architecture pairs at the canonical 70/30 cells, within-family-tie pairs at the headache TabPFN headline, cross-architecture pairs at the 70/15/15 ratio, the migraine XGBoost HP-budget ladder, and AutoTabPFN-vs-XGBoost load-bearing pairs (rather than selected from a larger candidate pool). Bonferroni FWER correction was applied to the full all-pairs cross-cell family for sensitivity. The contrast scope is frozen at Git tag `analysis-plan-frozen`, pointing to the single commit in the paired-DeLong evaluation script that introduced the 18-test structure; the file has exactly one commit in its history, so the contrast list has not been modified since.

Disclosure timing: the headline-cell leaf models were trained ~12-14 days before this freeze (migraine XGB-HP020 on 2026-05-15, headache TabPFN-v2.6 on 2026-05-13), so cross-architecture AUROC magnitudes were visible during contrast-list design. The paired DeLong p-values themselves were not computed until after the freeze. No formal pre-registration (OSF / PROSPERO / ClinicalTrials.gov entry) exists per the secondary-data-reanalysis design; we use "documented" rather than "pre-specified" throughout to acknowledge this distinction. This visibility window structurally undercuts strict FDR control, since contrast-list design occurred with AUROC magnitudes already known even though paired DeLong p-values were not. The 0-of-166-pair Bonferroni null reported below is the conservative summary against this structural caveat.

Paired DeLong assumes within-class independence; on patient-day data this assumption is violated by within-patient clustering. We do not assert a cohort-specific within-patient co-clustering regime or a directional claim about the inflation of Type-I error on this cohort; the methodological literature treats the direction of inflation under cluster violation as data-specific rather than as a known directional bias. The 0-of-166-pair Bonferroni null at the canonical chronological headline cells is reported as a conservative summary against this assumption violation. The §3.2 / §3.4 / §3.7 headline-cell metrics are already reported under the patient-cluster primary discipline that addresses within-patient serial dependence directly. Tie handling on post-Platt calibrated outputs used midrank averaging.

## Results

### Cohort flow and base rates

The analytic cohort is 62 patients across two Korean neurology clinics, yielding 4,516 patient-days at a 7.2% pooled migraine positive rate (Uijeongbu 8.6% vs Dongtan 5.7%). The Park 2016 enrolment funnel started at 113 patients recruited at the two clinics, with 30 patients withdrawing before the end of the study and 21 patients excluded for diary adherence below 50%, leaving the 62-patient Park 2016 analytic cohort (Figure 1, upstream funnel). Those 62 enrolled patients yielded 63 unique processed `patient_id` values after diary deduplication (the 63rd patient_id is a disability-sheet-only reconciliation patient with 38 patient-days, excluded from the site-mapping analysis but retained in the patient-day total) and 4,516 patient-days at 7.2% pooled migraine positive rate (Uijeongbu 8.6% vs Dongtan 5.7%); the 4,516 patient-day total is 63 days lower than the 4,579 diary days Park 2016 reports for the same n = 62 cohort because the next-day target `migraine_today.shift(-1)` is undefined on each patient's last observed day and so those 63 final-day rows are dropped from the analysis sample (one per processed `patient_id`). Forty-nine patients had estimable per-patient lag-1 autocorrelation for the migraine target; 62 patients had estimable autocorrelation for the headache target. ### Headline discrimination per Addition

On chronological splits the headline migraine AUROC reached 0.793 (95% CI 0.544-0.890, patient-cluster primary) and the headline headache AUROC reached 0.653 (95% CI 0.556-0.740, patient-cluster primary); the within-person C-statistic clustered at 0.53-0.57 across architectures and both targets (Figure 4; §3.6). The chronological split is patient-overlapping by design (patients contribute rows to train, val, and test through different date windows; §2.4), so these AUROCs reflect within-patient temporal generalisation rather than held-out-patient transport, distinct from the within-person day-ranking framing of §3.6. The headline migraine leaf is the XGB-HP020 stack at `full_features` 70/30, the pre-declared under-powered cell per §2.1 (patient-day sensitivity CI 0.701-0.873). The headline headache leaf is the TabPFN family at `full / chrono / 70/30`: v2.6, v3-default, and v3-binary tied within the composite rule's 0.02 AUROC tier (§2.5), v2.6 picked by the calibration-distance tiebreak (patient-day sensitivity CI 0.590-0.715).

As a Holsteen-construct-equivalence sensitivity (per §2.1), the `no_rolling_features` cell removes the engineered history channel (rolling means, streaks, lag features) on the predictor side, which is the in-pipeline mechanical analog of Holsteen et al. 2020's [4] Recovery-day exclusion on the target side. On chronological 70/30 the headline migraine AUROC drops from 0.793 (XGB-HP020 stack at `full_features`) to 0.619 (95% CI 0.520-0.716, XGBoost stack non-HP at `no_rolling_features`), and the headline headache AUROC drops from 0.653 (TabPFN-v2.6 at `full_features`) to 0.579 (95% CI 0.517-0.637, TabPFN-v2.6 at `no_rolling_features`). The migraine drop of 0.174 closely approximates the 0.23 pooled-vs-within gap; the headache drop of 0.074 closely approximates the 0.11 gap. The pooled-minus-within gap is therefore traceable to the lag-1 history channel that the target-side Recovery-day exclusion would also remove. SHAP and ALE attribution at the `no_rolling_features` cell (Supplementary Figure S-NR1) and the flexible loess calibration smooth at the same cell (Supplementary Figure S-NR2) are reported alongside the §3 Figure 2 and Figure 3 panels at the `full_features` headline cells. The migraine/no_rolling/patient hold-out cell is also where the single BH-FDR survivor lives (see Bonferroni/BH-FDR paragraph below), so the construct-equivalence reading re-contextualises that survivor as a cross-architecture contrast at a cell whose pooled AUROC has already absorbed the construct-equivalence demotion.

The central claim of this benchmark rests on the effect-size magnitude of the pooled-minus-within gap (0.23 migraine; 0.11 headache) rather than on p-value survival, but the cross-architecture comparison family is reported for transparency. Under conservative Bonferroni FWER correction across 166 architecture pairs at the canonical chronological headline cells, 0 contrasts achieved significance, the defensible cross-architecture finding and the protected summary against the §2.8 visibility caveat. Under less-conservative BH-FDR correction across the 18 documented contrasts named in §2.8, 1 survived at q ≤ 0.05 (migraine/no_rolling/patient cell at 70/30: TabPFN-v3-binary vs `stacked_2xgb_NonHP`, ΔAUROC +0.103, p = 0.002, q = 0.036), a cross-architecture contrast at the small-cohort sparse patient hold-out cell rather than at the canonical chronological headline cells. ### Stratified-split optimism is feature-channel leakage

Stratified splits inflated AUROC only on feature sets with history features (`full_features`, `spano_features`). Feature sets without history (`no_rolling_features`, `park_features`) showed no inflation; stratified was in fact slightly worse than chronological for those cells. SHAP attribution corroborated the leakage channel by mean absolute |SHAP| (sign-invariant, so unaffected by the Platt-inversion cells in §3.4): the history features (`migraine_rate_last7`, `headache_free_streak`, `days_since_last_migraine`) carried 77 to 85% of mean absolute attribution on `full_features` chronological cells and gained both rank and magnitude under stratification (e.g. `migraine_rate_last7` rose from rank 3 to rank 1 on the headache stratified cell, a 2.4-fold magnitude shift; Figure 2 shows the per-row SHAP beeswarms at the two chronological-headline cells, with the history features occupying the top rows of both panels and the trigger features clustered tightly around zero below them). The chronological split is the methodologically honest evaluation; stratified is reported only to quantify the leakage channel. Supplementary Figure S2 (`fig_c1_leakage.py`) decomposes the stratified-minus-chronological AUROC inflation by history-feature count.

### Calibration is fragile on the small or sparse cells

The median cross-cell calibration slope was 0.64, indicating over-confidence. On the headline cells, the migraine slope was 1.386 (95% CI 0.401-2.067 patient-cluster primary, 0.972-1.924 patient-day sensitivity) and the headache slope was 1.094 (95% CI 0.594-1.429 patient-cluster, 0.764-1.453 patient-day sensitivity). Both patient-cluster CIs included 1.0, with wider margins than the patient-day reading suggested, and the migraine point estimate sat materially above unity.

Calibration-in-the-large (CITL = mean(y) / mean(p), with 1.0 indicating no systematic bias) was 1.095 patient-cluster [0.554, 1.782] for migraine XGBoost, 1.140 [0.619, 1.591] for migraine TabPFN, 1.025 [0.805, 1.305] for headache XGBoost, and 0.978 [0.800, 1.190] for headache TabPFN. All four headline CITL CIs contained 1.0 under both bootstrap units, so the headline cells were not systematically over- or under-forecasting at the cohort level. Patient-day sensitivity CIs are reported per architecture in the supplementary materials.

Flexible (loess) calibration smooths at the two headline cells with patient-cluster bootstrap 95% bands (Figure 3) extend this weak-calibration reporting to the moderate-calibration level. The per-architecture smooths show how the observed positive rate tracks predicted probability across the data range. A per-architecture predicted-probability rug at the bottom of each panel marks where the smooth is data-supported versus sparse-data-extrapolated at the high-p edge.

On the migraine `full_features / 70_30 / chrono` cell, the 488-cell-grid sourcing means the comparator CIs are patient-day-iid (the patient-cluster discipline is concentrated on the headline cells, not the broader grid; see §2.8). AutoTabPFN (`version_2-5-auto`) reached patient-day-iid calibration slope 0.992 [0.642, 1.346]. This is closer to 1.0 than both the migraine XGB-HP020 headline slope of 1.386 (reported under patient-cluster primary) and the migraine TabPFN-v2.5-finetuned patient-day-iid slope of 0.964 [0.604, 1.346]. All three migraine slope CIs included 1.0 within their respective bootstrap units, so the architectures did not separate at the CI level despite the point-estimate ordering. AutoTabPFN patient-day-iid AUROC 0.745 [0.656, 0.825] sat within the XGB-HP020 patient-day-iid AUROC CI [0.701, 0.873].

Across the 488-cell grid, 98 cells had a negative slope: the Platt-inversion fingerprint of small-calibration-set overfitting. The Platt sigmoid's fitted slope coefficient comes out negative when the calibration sub-split's logit-label relationship inverts on the held-out test set, exposing HP-search-induced overfitting in leakage-prone splits; the model itself is not inverted, the calibrator is. The pattern concentrated in HP-tuned XGBoost on stratified splits (where the inner CV's calibration sub-split is statistically thin) and on the sparse migraine patient-hold-out cells. TabPFN's single-forward-pass in-context inference did not exhibit this collapse: of 42 anti-predictive cells (hold-out AUROC < 0.45) across the 488-cell grid, 38 were `stacked_2xgb_meta_lr` and only four were TabPFN, all marginal.

The calibration framing is the methodological reason this study reports both discrimination and calibration; reporting AUC alone hides the cells where the meta-learner's posterior collapses. The full calibration-slope distribution across the 488-cell grid against the 1.0 reference line is provided as Supplementary Figure S6 (`fig_g3_calib_slope.py`). Calibration reporting is necessary because pooled AUROC alone hides the within-person collapse documented at §3.6.

### Temporal dependence justifies a sequence-model test but not its dominance

The daily attack series carried strong short-range within-patient serial dependence in both targets, confirmed by four independent methods. This battery answers a load-bearing falsification question: if sequence baselines cannot break the within-person near-chance ceiling reported at §3.6, the within-person floor is regime-level rather than architecture-level. The pooled lag-1 autocorrelation was 0.267 for migraine and 0.218 for headache; the trigger-controlled discrete-time self-excitation logistic regression gave a lag-1 odds ratio of 5.43 for migraine (LR vs triggers-only χ² = 155, p = 2.5e-33) and 2.44 for headache (χ² = 203, p = 1.1e-43); the first-order Markov transition P(attack tomorrow | attack today) was 0.315 for migraine versus 0.050 without (risk ratio 6.3). Goh-Barabasi burstiness B [19] clustered near zero (median -0.05 migraine, -0.06 headache), so the dependence was short-range day-to-day clustering rather than long-tailed bursty memory, consistent with Houle et al. 2005 [3] and Barra et al. 2020 [18]. The sequence baselines (window-MLP, GRU, 1D-CNN) did not unseat the XGBoost-stack headline on the chronological full_features cells. The verdict was "competitive but not dominant" rather than a uniform null: the short-range temporal-signal prediction was supported, but the engineered lag and rolling features in the tabular models already encoded the recoverable signal. Cross-architecture differences at the val+test holdout horizon (n = 575 patient-days) were not statistically separable: every architecture's bootstrap 95% AUROC CI contained the other four architectures' point estimates in both targets (Supplementary Figure S3), so the verdict held on the CI structure as well as on the serial-dependence theory. This is consistent with the §3.6 finding that within-person ranking is near chance: the recoverable temporal signal sat in the engineered lag features, not in a sequence model's hidden state.

### Within-person evaluation: pooled AUROC overstates within-person C by 0.23 (migraine) and 0.11 (headache)

The within-person C-statistic clustered at 0.53 to 0.57 for every architecture and both targets, near chance and architecture-independent. The headline migraine XGB-HP020 cell sits at 0.558 [0.511, 0.604] and the headline headache TabPFN-v2.6 at 0.542 [0.509, 0.575]. Per-architecture Paule-Mandel within-person C ranged 0.530-0.558 for migraine and 0.538-0.543 for headache on 19/63 and 57/63 estimable patients at the principled five-positive floor. The cross-architecture set (XGBoost stack, TabPFN, window-MLP sequence) sits within 0.028 of itself on migraine and within 0.005 on headache.

This measurement used out-of-fold cross-validation on the non-hyperparameter-tuned 70/30 chronological leaves. HP-tuned leaves were excluded because their calibrated probabilities carry the Platt-inversion fragility documented in §3.4. DerSimonian-Laird sensitivity on the headline TabPFN cells reads 0.542 [0.508, 0.576] for headache and 0.563 [0.500, 0.626] for migraine, agreeing with Paule-Mandel at the third decimal.

The per-patient AUROC distribution centres on chance and sits visibly below the pooled AUROC line in both targets at the two non-hyperparameter-tuned 70/30 chronological cells (Figure 4, sorted by patient against the pooled AUROC and Hanley-McNeil within-person C reference lines), the visual analogue of the pooled-minus-within gap reported below. Within-person calibration at the headline cells (Figure 5) extends this discrimination measurement with its calibration analogue: when each patient's predicted-probability days are sorted into within-patient quartiles, the per-quartile pooled observed positive rate runs roughly flat across Q1-Q4 (headache 0.21-0.32 across architectures, migraine 0.13-0.23) while the per-quartile pooled predicted probability spans 0.14-0.41 (headache) and 0.03-0.27 (migraine). Within-patient ranking by predicted probability therefore does not separate the per-quartile observed rates: the within-person calibration analogue of the near-chance within-person C.

Stratum disaggregation of the within-person C by site, sex, and per-patient base-rate stratum (Figure 6; full CSV in the released code repository) confirms the pooled near-chance result survives the disaggregation. Each stratum with at least the 5-patient reporting floor carries a within-person C in the 0.50-0.60 range with mutually overlapping CIs; the largest cross-stratum gap was TabPFN migraine Uijeongbu 0.593 [0.506, 0.681] versus Dongtan 0.522 [0.447, 0.596]. Two migraine strata sit below the reporting floor and are surfaced quantitatively rather than collapsed into the pooled view: the male stratum (n = 1 estimable patient of 11 male patients enrolled cohort-wide) and the below-7.2%-base-rate stratum (n = 3 estimable patients), both insufficient for a within-person C estimate with non-degenerate CI.

The pooled-minus-within gap reached 0.173 AUROC for migraine TabPFN (pooled 0.738 → within-person 0.565 Paule-Mandel; pooled 0.738 → within-person 0.563 DerSimonian-Laird, gap 0.175), the largest gap in the sweep regardless of the τ² estimator choice. The within-person architecture differences sat inside overlapping CIs, statistically indistinguishable per patient. Holsteen et al. 2020 reported within-person C 0.56 [0.54, 0.58] on US n = 178 under their new-onset-only target; we observe 0.542 [0.509, 0.575] (headache) and 0.558 [0.511, 0.604] (migraine) on Korean n = 62 under an any-next-day target, directionally consistent with the Holsteen within-person near-chance pattern despite the construct difference noted at §2.1 [4].

### Clinical value: forecast quality versus forecast value

Headache Brier skill was positive only on the TabPFN-v2.6 headline cell under patient-cluster bootstrap: +0.215 [+0.017, +0.396], the only architecture with a 95% CI fully above zero. XGBoost +0.198 [-0.017, +0.385] and the window-MLP sequence baseline +0.139 [-0.123, +0.362] carry patient-cluster CIs that cross zero. The patient-day-iid bootstrap had read all three architectures as positive; under the patient-cluster discipline this collapses to a TabPFN-v2.6-specific finding consistent with §2.5's composite rule. The TabPFN-v2.6 result is itself conditional on the §2.5 composite-rule calibration-distance tiebreak selecting v2.6 from a three-way AUROC tie at this cell, so it should not be over-interpreted as architecture-level evidence (see §4.4 Sixth limitation). The headache target captures the broader same-day headache-presence flag (1,099 of 4,516 patient-days, of which 336 met ICHD-3 migraine criteria and 763 did not, per §2.1) rather than ICHD-3-adjudicated migraine specifically; pre-emptive abortive medications (triptans, gepants, CGRP receptor antagonists) are licensed for migraine specifically, so the headache Brier-skill result does not by itself establish migraine-pre-emptive clinical actionability. The same target construct ("any headache" rather than ICHD-3 migraine specifically) is the published Cephalalgia precedent in Stubberud et al. 2023 [23], where the authors disclose that "not distinguishing between headache attacks is likely to have hampered predictions as migraine and non-migraine attacks have varying premonitory symptoms and physiological properties"; the present manuscript follows the same in-field disclosure practice.

Migraine is at most marginally negative. Only the sequence baseline carries a CI fully below zero (-0.117 [-0.241, -0.026] patient-cluster). The XGBoost migraine CI includes zero (-0.068 [-0.257, +0.077]) and the TabPFN migraine CI includes zero (-0.057 [-0.172, +0.063]), despite migraine pooled AUROC ranging from 0.738 to 0.793 across architectures. High migraine discrimination therefore did not translate into significant probabilistic value beyond the per-patient base-rate forecast. The migraine tabular-architecture skill was statistically indistinguishable from the climatology baseline rather than significantly worse: this is Murphy's quality-versus-value distinction with finite-sample uncertainty acknowledged [17].

Decision-curve net benefit on the migraine target (Figure 7, panel b) sat near zero across the documented threshold band (0.01-0.50). Patient-cluster bootstrap 95% CI bands included zero at every threshold for all three architectures (XGBoost stack +0.035 [-0.005, +0.105] at t = 0.05, TabPFN +0.033 [-0.005, +0.101], window-MLP +0.034 [-0.006, +0.102]).

The headache architectures (Figure 7, panel a) dominated both the treat-all and treat-none reference policies across the same band at the point-estimate level (net benefit +0.05 to +0.20). Patient-cluster bootstrap 95% CI bands sat fully above zero only in the clinically plausible low-threshold sub-band (XGBoost stack +0.152 [+0.094, +0.234] at t = 0.05, TabPFN +0.152 [+0.094, +0.234], window-MLP +0.146 [+0.087, +0.230]; all three CI bands fully above zero throughout t = 0.01-0.10). The patient-cluster CI lower edges cross zero from t = 0.20 (window-MLP first at -0.027), and all three cross zero by t = 0.30. The headache decision-curve verdict under the patient-cluster discipline therefore holds at the clinically plausible low-threshold band rather than throughout the full 0.01-0.50 evaluation range that a patient-day-iid bootstrap reading reports as a wider apparent band of dominance.

Under the Vickers 2019 dual-comparator criterion, the relevant reference policy depends on the threshold. Below the headache prevalence (approximately 0.24) treat-all is the binding comparator; above it, treat-none is. The CI-crosses-zero framing from t = 0.20 onwards therefore reflects loss of dominance over treat-none and loss of dominance over the increasingly-negative treat-all at moderate thresholds, rather than loss of usefulness against a single reference.

Stubberud et al. 2023 [23] note as a Discussion-paragraph advisory that "a high forecasting accuracy (AUC > 0.80) would be required for individuals with high-frequency headache to avoid medication overuse headache, because a low specificity would increase the rate of false positive drug administrations": a conditional applying to a high-frequency-headache subpopulation under a MOH-avoidance scenario, not a general migraine-forecasting publication threshold. Anchored against that conditional, the headline migraine pooled AUROC 0.793 sits at the boundary, while the within-person C 0.558 sits well below it; the MOH-constrained deployment criterion is not met on this cohort regardless of the discrimination metric chosen.

These decision-curve net-benefit CIs at the clinically plausible low-threshold sub-band are reported alongside the §3.4 slope and CITL CIs as part of the Riley / Martin 2025 [12] four-criterion validation discipline (discrimination, calibration slope, CITL, and decision-curve net benefit). The headline cells' validation is reported on every Riley-school precision axis.

### Within-study cross-clinic robustness: calibration drift tracks the inter-site base-rate gap

Leave-one-site-out validation across Uijeongbu (8.6% migraine) and Dongtan (5.7%) reproduced the within-person near-chance result off-site: migraine within-person C ranged from 0.457 [0.359, 0.556] (XGBoost stack, Uijeongbu held out) to 0.577 [0.490, 0.663] (TabPFN, Uijeongbu held out) across the eight architecture-by-site cells, with the XGBoost cell sitting below 0.50; headache within-person C ranged from 0.488 [0.442, 0.535] (window-MLP, Dongtan held out) to 0.586 [0.533, 0.639] (TabPFN, Uijeongbu held out). Pooled discrimination held across sites: migraine AUROC ranged from 0.622 [0.582, 0.665] (XGBoost stack, Uijeongbu held out) to 0.785 [0.743, 0.827] (TabPFN, Dongtan held out); headache AUROC ranged from 0.567 [0.538, 0.596] (window-MLP, Dongtan held out) to 0.670 [0.646, 0.697] (TabPFN, Uijeongbu held out). Calibration drifted with the site base rate (Figure 8): a model carried from the 8.6% site to the 5.7% site over-predicted (O:E fell to 0.50; on the maximally-drifting window-MLP cell the held-out 2-parameter Platt refit returned intercept α = -1.52 [-1.82, -1.22] and slope 0.55 [0.42, 0.71]); carried the other way it under-predicted (O:E rose to 1.54; on the maximally-drifting XGBoost-stack cell the refit returned α = +2.41 [+1.35, +3.62] and slope 0.37 [0.26, 0.49]). The reported (α, β) come from the joint 2-parameter Platt refit on the held-out site; by construction the joint refit recovers both mean and slope calibration on the test data, the joint α encodes the logit-scale shift the cross-clinic miscalibration demanded under simultaneous slope correction, and the below-unity slope values show that even after intercept adjustment the predictions remain over-confident, with the joint refit absorbing the residual over-confidence into the slope term. An alternative intercept-only Platt refit (β fixed at 1) would also recover mean calibration on the held-out site, with a smaller |α| but no slope correction. The TabPFN family was the calibration-robust outlier: |joint α| sat near zero in both transport directions (Uijeongbu→Dongtan: α = +0.26 [-0.19, +0.72], slope 1.14 [0.96, 1.34]; Dongtan→Uijeongbu: α = -0.16 [-0.57, +0.26], slope 0.76 [0.61, 0.93]), consistent with the meta-trained prior delivering calibrated probabilities under mild cohort shift [27]. The mean predicted risk tracked the training site, not the test site. No in-study model updating or recalibration was performed. Deployment to a new site would require site-specific recalibration on a local sample before clinical use; the present benchmark does not estimate the local-sample size required to achieve calibration slope and CITL within tolerated bounds at a new site. Within-person C across the two recruitment sites is provided as Supplementary Figure S8 (`fig_e2_within_person_sites.py`).

## Discussion

### The pooled-AUROC framing overstates within-person value on this cohort

In this study, we developed and internally validated a multivariable next-day forecasting benchmark for episodic migraine and headache on the Park 2016 Korean smartphone diary cohort (62 patients; 4,516 patient-days; 7.2% pooled migraine positive rate), contrasting pooled with within-person discrimination as the load-bearing endpoint. Pooled AUROC overstated within-person discrimination by a point-estimate gap of 0.23 (migraine) and 0.11 (headache), with the within-person C-statistic clustering near chance (0.53-0.57) across architectures and both targets (headache TabPFN-v2.6 0.542 [0.509, 0.575]; migraine XGB-HP020 0.558 [0.511, 0.604]), so the benchmarked models do not support individual-patient day-by-day clinical use on diary-only inputs at this cohort size. Pooled discrimination on this cohort reflects between-patient base-rate separation rather than within-person day-to-day ranking. Translated into the deployment-relevance discipline: Stubberud et al. 2023 note as a Discussion-paragraph advisory that "a high forecasting accuracy (AUC > 0.80) would be required for individuals with high-frequency headache to avoid medication overuse headache, because a low specificity would increase the rate of false positive drug administrations" [23], a conditional applying to a high-frequency-headache subpopulation under a MOH-avoidance scenario rather than a general migraine-forecasting publication threshold. Anchored against that conditional, the present cohort's headline pooled migraine AUROC 0.793 sits at the boundary while the within-person C 0.558 sits well below it, so the MOH-constrained deployment criterion is not met regardless of the discrimination metric chosen. A forecast that beats chance pooled does not necessarily let a given patient act on *their* day-by-day predictions: the present pooled-AUROC magnitude does not translate into actionable individual-patient guidance under this cohort size and diary-only input regime. This is directionally consistent with the within-person near-chance pattern reported by Holsteen et al. 2020 [4] on an independent US cohort under a stricter new-onset-only target, despite the §2.1 construct difference; the diary-only forecasting regime sits in the AUROC 0.56 to 0.65 band the field reports under within-person evaluation, well below the wearable-augmented high-water mark.

### Four converging lines of evidence support the pooled-overstates finding

Four converging lines of evidence support the pooled-overstates conclusion. The **personalisation evaluation** produces the load-bearing within-person C-statistic measurement and shows that partial pooling's pooled-AUROC gain does not survive the within-person reframing. The **temporal-dependence battery** quantifies the within-patient serial dependence directly and predicts that any sequence-model gain will be small, because the recoverable signal sits in a short window that the engineered lag and rolling features already encode. The **sequence-model falsification test** confirms that prediction: no sequence variant beat the XGBoost stack headline. The **within-study cross-clinic robustness** check shows that pooled-vs-within architecture rankings hold across the two participating clinics but with calibration drift consistent with between-patient base-rate separation driving the pooled metric. These four lines are not statistically independent: the temporal-dependence battery motivates the sequence-model falsification test, and the personalisation evaluation produces (rather than corroborates) the within-person C measurement itself. They nonetheless converge from different methodological angles on the pooled-overstates reading.

Architecture-quality findings reported alongside (the calibration fragility of HP-tuned XGBoost stacking on small calibration sets, the robustness of TabPFN under small calibration sets, and the SHAP/ALE corroboration of the stratified-split leakage mechanism) describe model behaviour but do not directly measure the pooled-vs-within gap and are not counted as load-bearing lines of evidence for the pooled-overstates finding.

### Field implications

Three nearest comparators frame this work. Holsteen et al. 2020 used a daily trigger diary in 178 episodic migraineurs and reported a within-person C-statistic of 0.56 (95% CI 0.54-0.58), close to the 0.542 (headache) and 0.558 (migraine) we observe here [4]. Stubberud et al. 2023 linked diary data with wearable physiology in 18 episodic migraineurs and reached held-out AUC 0.62 [23]. Faisal et al. 2026 used trapezius EMG, HRV, and skin-temperature wearables across 21,550 headache days, reaching AUC 0.84 with a GRU-D time-series architecture, while their standard-ML and TabPFN architectures reached only 0.55-0.59 on the same inputs [6]. Faisal's TabPFN at 0.55 next-day pooled AUC, on roughly five times this cohort's patient-day count with HRV + EMG + skin-temperature enrichment, is consistent with the §3.4 falsification reading that the within-person ceiling on tabular daily-aggregated migraine forecasting is regime-bound rather than architecture-bound: the regime shifts when a sequence architecture meets denser physiological inputs at larger scale, not when a tabular foundation model meets either modality alone. The present diary-only Korean cohort sits within the band these comparators establish, and the within-person near-chance result reproduces independently of architecture choice. None of these comparators cites a prediction-model reporting checklist by name; we adopt TRIPOD+AI [7] + PROBAST+AI [8] as the explicit reporting standard for diary-only migraine forecasting.

Of the methodological directions named in §1, the present benchmark closes three: the within-person C-statistic reporting gap that Stubberud et al. 2024 [34] flags as an open methodological direction, the pooled-vs-within reframe for diary-only Korean SHD data that the Lee/Chu 2025 systematic review [33] identified as absent from the AI-migraine-attack-prediction literature, and the calibration + decision-curve + Brier-skill alongside discrimination discipline that Petrušić et al. 2024 [9] criteria 4-6 demand. Three remain open and motivate the Conclusion's three-lever prescription: wearable augmentation, multi-cohort scale, and phenotype-homogeneous subsetting.

Will patients benefit more from knowing which of their days will carry an attack, or from knowing which of their days will not? Four implications for the diary-only migraine-forecasting subfield follow.

First (binding), calibration slope, decision-curve net benefit, and a within-person C-statistic should be reported alongside pooled discrimination, so that papers can be compared on the metric matching the clinical use case rather than on pooled AUROC alone.

Second (binding), AUROC alone is insufficient when the clinical action is a probability-thresholded pre-emptive medication decision: the migraine target reaches pooled AUROC 0.738 to 0.793 across architectures yet returns negative Brier skill against each patient's own climatology.

Third (research agenda), the largest open trigger-diary cohort in the public record, the Park 2016 SHD dataset, is almost entirely unexploited for machine learning. This work provides published reference points for the diary-only forecasting regime that prior wearable-augmented work cannot supply.

Fourth (binding), future SHD-class diary studies should adopt the within-person C-statistic as the per-day-claim-eligibility gate: any pooled-AUROC headline should be paired with a within-person C reading at the same headline cell, so that day-level claims are anchored to the within-person evidence rather than to between-patient base-rate separation alone. Can a diary-only forecast support a pre-emptive abortive decision at all without wearable-derived autonomic or hormonal channels? This is the clinically actionable question the within-person reframing makes answerable; the present benchmark indicates the diary-only regime does not yet meet that bar on this cohort.

The biologically privileged trigger in the source cohort is hormonal change: estrogen fluctuation across the menstrual cycle influences migraine occurrence via cellular excitability and cerebral vasculature [31]. Park 2016 identified hormonal change as one of two triggers (the other being noise) increasing migraine risk independently of preventive medication, and it carried the second-largest stepwise-selected odds ratio in the Park 2016 Table 4 logistic regression (OR 3.5, 95% CI 2.3-5.2) [2]. The present benchmark's SHAP and ALE attribution recovers a positive effect direction for hormonal change in line with Park's univariate sign, but the SHD diary instrument records hormonal change as a binary same-day flag rather than as a menstrual-cycle phase-resolved index; the trigger-level recovery here therefore remains at the effect-direction level. The cohort is 82.3% female aged 19-55, the demographic window in which menstrually-related migraine (ICHD-3 appendix A1.1.2) is clinically distinct, and the cyclical menstrual-phase predictor is precisely what the binary same-day flag necessarily collapses. The cycle-phase limitation is therefore not a generic instrument limitation but a removal of the strongest known cyclical predictor for a substantial cohort subset.

### Strengths and limitations

Strengths of the present benchmark include a documented evaluation regime, calibration reported alongside discrimination on every cell, within-person C-statistic and decision-curve net benefit on the canonical leaves, leave-one-site-out validation across the two recruitment clinics, and open code and data.

Seven limitations bind on the interpretation of these results. First, the cohort is small for machine learning (n = 62 patients, 7.2% positive rate; migraine `full_features` EPV 5.5) and the only public extension, the Park-group 82-patient SHED, was never deposited, so no separate-cohort external validation is currently feasible [12]. Second, the chronological split is patient-overlapping by design and the leave-one-site-out check does not meet HTA-framework external-validation criteria (which require separate-cohort transport with different protocols or geographic settings), so PROBAST+AI applicability is narrowed to within-study cross-clinic robustness. Third, primary-care deployment scope is constrained because the 62 enrolled patients were specialist-clinic recruited at two Korean neurology services with 21 of 113 originally-recruited patients excluded for sub-50% diary adherence, so primary-care case-mix prevalence and non-study adherence regimes are not estimated by the present internal validation. Fourth, sex-stratified deployment cannot be evaluated because the n = 11 male enrolment yields one estimable male stratum patient [23], and the SHD diary instrument has not undergone separate formal psychometric validation in a non-Korean population. Fifth, the phenotype-mixing sensitivity per Petrušić criterion 1 [9] shows that excluding the single test-set aura patient (one of n = 2 enrolled) shifts headline migraine AUROC modestly within CI while the cohort-level conclusions survive the exclusion. Sixth, the positive headache Brier skill on TabPFN-v2.6 is conditional on the §2.5 composite-rule calibration-distance tiebreak selecting v2.6 from a three-way AUROC tie (v2.6, v3-default, v3-binary), so the result is not independent cross-architecture evidence. Finally, fairness across underrepresented strata is partially testable: the pooled within-person C near 0.55 survives across strata meeting the 5-patient reporting floor, but the migraine male and below-7.2%-base-rate strata sit below it and are surfaced quantitatively rather than collapsed.

### Future work

Four next directions follow.

First, out-of-cohort external validation on the Park-group 82-patient SHED cohort [20, 21], gated on a data-sharing request to the original authors. SHED was recruited September 2014 to May 2015 at the same two clinics under a broader inclusion frame than Park 2016: Park 2018 uses age 19-65 versus Park 2016's 19-55, so a validation step would entail an age-band shift in addition to the cohort shift.

Second, wearable augmentation toward the Faisal 2026 band [6], positioned against prior diary-plus-wearable [23], per-patient ambulatory hemodynamic forecasting at minutes-ahead horizons [25], and overnight-wearable migraine detection [26].

Third, multi-cohort deep personalisation, once a second cohort is in hand.

Fourth, disability-driven analysis using the unused SHD disability sheet, which records binary disability flags per headache day across three life-domain categories (work or school, housework, and social engagements) with severe and moderate degree levels for the work and housework domains and a single binary flag for the social domain. This would test whether the within-person near-chance result reproduces on disability targets rather than on the binary attack flag, and whether disability-stratified modelling supports a distinct clinically actionable claim. Attack frequency alone explains only a moderate fraction of migraine-related disability, and psychological transdiagnostic factors (pain catastrophizing, fear of attacks, depressiveness) contribute substantially beyond migraine symptoms on HIT-6 disability variance in episodic migraine [32]; disability impact is the proximal outcome a patient and clinician care about, and a forecast that predicts attack presence near chance may yet predict disability incidence at a different precision floor.

The present benchmark does not evaluate deployment-stage usability; the relevant reporting guideline for that stage is DECIDE-AI [22].

## Conclusion

On a single 62-patient Korean smartphone-diary cohort, pooled AUROC overstated within-person discrimination by 0.23 (migraine) and 0.11 (headache); the within-person C-statistic clustered at 0.53-0.57 across architectures and both targets. Headache forecasts retained probabilistic value on the TabPFN-v2.6 headline cell (Brier skill +0.215 patient-cluster), where v2.6 was selected by the §2.5 composite-rule tiebreak from a three-way TabPFN AUROC tie (v2.6, v3-default, v3-binary) and the headache target captures the broader same-day headache-presence flag rather than ICHD-3-adjudicated migraine, so the result is neither independent architecture-level evidence nor a migraine-specific clinical-actionability finding; migraine forecasts returned negative Brier skill against per-patient climatology. We do not recommend clinical deployment without out-of-cohort transport. Future diary-only forecasting work should report calibration slope, decision-curve net benefit, and a within-person C-statistic alongside pooled discrimination, so that day-level claims rest on the within-person evidence the clinical use case demands. Substantive improvements likely require three converging levers: wearable augmentation of the input channel toward the Faisal 2026 band [6], larger multi-cohort scale toward the Holsteen 2020 n = 178 range [4], and phenotype-homogeneous subsetting per the Petrušić 2024 criterion 1 [9]; each closes a specific gap this benchmark cannot address from diary-only Park 2016 data alone.

## Clinical implications

**In plain terms.** A within-person C-statistic of 0.55 means our forecast correctly orders a patient's randomly chosen attack day above a randomly chosen non-attack day only about 55% of the time, barely better than a coin flip; a useful day-by-day decision aid would need this number above roughly 0.70. Brier skill compares the forecast against simply using each patient's own historical attack rate as the baseline; positive Brier skill means the forecast adds information beyond climatology. Calibration slope ≈ 1 means predicted probabilities match observed rates, and EPV 5.5 flags the migraine `full_features` cell as under-powered relative to the conventional events-per-variable threshold near 20.

- Pooled AUROC on smartphone-diary cohorts does not by itself establish day-by-day forecast utility for individual patients; pair it with a within-person C-statistic before reading it as evidence of individual-patient utility.
- Diary-only forecasting at the present cohort scale does not yet support a per-patient pre-emptive abortive medication decision; wearable augmentation or a substantially larger multi-cohort dataset are the substantive levers that may move the within-person C above the diary-only floor reproduced here.
