# Within-person versus pooled discrimination in next-day migraine and headache prediction models on the Park 2016 SHD cohort

> Position: **JHP body**. ~4000-word submission body synthesising the long-form record under `docs/`. The per-Addition methods, results, and TRIPOD+AI compliance trail live in the long-form docs and are referenced from here; nothing in the body is novel relative to those records.

## Abstract

**Background.** Pooled AUROC for daily migraine forecasting conflates within-person day-to-day ranking with between-patient base-rate separation, leaving clinical utility unresolved. The diary-only literature sits in AUROC 0.56 to 0.73 under within-person evaluation, well below wearable-augmented work (up to 0.84) using physiological inputs the diary cohort does not carry.

**Methods.** Next-day migraine and headache forecasting were evaluated on the Park 2016 smartphone-headache-diary cohort (62 patients; 4,516 patient-days; 7.2% positive rate; two Korean clinics; 19-55 y; 82.3% female; single ethnicity). Three architecture families were compared across three feature sets, three split types (chronological/stratified/patient hold-out), and two ratios (70/30, 70/15/15): XGBoost stacking (with/without 500-trial Optuna HP search), TabPFN (five variants, default configuration; no per-variant HPO), and a window-MLP sequence baseline. Missing days were structural; no imputation. Headline cells were selected by a composite rule gating on calibration slope; the migraine `full_features` headline cell carries EPV 5.5 against the Riley/Ensor/Martin criterion (high-risk overfitting band) and is reported throughout as a pre-declared under-powered development cell, not a discovery of inadequacy at evaluation time. External validation was geographic internal-external (leave-one-site-out across the two participating clinics of the same study). Discrimination, calibration, and 1,000-iteration bootstrap 95% CIs were reported together; cross-architecture comparisons used paired DeLong with Benjamini-Hochberg correction across 18 pre-specified contrasts, escalated to a 166-pair all-pairs Bonferroni sensitivity at the canonical headline cells (0 of 166 significant).

**Results.** The within-person C-statistic clustered at 0.53-0.57 across architectures and targets (TabPFN headache 0.542 [0.508-0.576], 57 of 63 estimable patients; TabPFN migraine 0.563 [0.500-0.626], 19 of 63 at the five-positive floor); per-patient AUROC centred near chance. Chronological-split pooled headline AUROC reached 0.793 (95% CI 0.701-0.873) for migraine (XGB-HP020) and 0.652 (0.589-0.712) for headache (TabPFN-v2.6, headline-tied with v3-default and v3-binary within a 0.02 AUROC tier, picked by calibration-distance tiebreak); pooled AUROC therefore exceeded within-person C by a margin attributable to between-patient base-rate separation, not day-to-day ranking. Headline calibration slopes were 1.417 [0.952-1.942] (migraine) and 1.095 [0.744-1.427] (headache). Brier skill against each patient's TRAIN-set climatology was significantly positive for headache across architectures (CIs +0.07 to +0.29) and significantly negative for migraine only on the sequence baseline (-0.117 [-0.227, -0.037]); migraine XGBoost and TabPFN CIs included zero. Leave-one-site-out validation showed calibration drift: observed-to-expected ratio fell to 0.50 when an 8.6%-prevalence site trained the model applied to the 5.7% site, and rose to 1.54 in the opposite direction.

**Conclusion.** Pooled AUROC is not a sufficient endpoint for the migraine target on diary-only data; a usable forecast requires richer per-patient signal or a re-framed target. This work reports development and internal validation on a single 62-patient cohort; we do not recommend clinical deployment.

## 1. Introduction

Migraine is the second-leading single cause of disability worldwide and the leading cause among young women aged 15-49 [steiner2020migraine, p. 1; p. 2], and its attacks carry day-level associations with same-day or recent-day exposures: stress, sleep disruption, hormonal change, weather, dietary triggers. A sufficiently accurate one-day-ahead probability is one route to converting reactive treatment into a pre-emptive pathway; whether diary-only inputs can reach the per-patient ranking accuracy a pre-emptive medication decision requires is the question this benchmark evaluates. The Korean Smartphone Headache Diary (SHD) cohort of Park et al. 2016 [park2016shd, p. 3] is the largest open next-day-diary migraine dataset that records the full trigger panel: 62 enrolled patients across two neurology clinics, 4,516 patient-days, and a daily attack indicator with eighteen candidate triggers recorded each evening.

The next-day-diary migraine-forecasting literature reports discrimination as a pooled AUROC computed across patient-days. Pooled AUROC mixes two distinct quantities: how well the model ranks days within a patient, and how well it separates high-base-rate patients from low-base-rate ones. Within-patient time-series methodology in headache research was first put on an empirical footing by Houle et al. 2005 [houle2005timeseries, pp. 445-446]; Holsteen et al. 2020 carried that framing into a modern forecasting evaluation and reported a within-person C-statistic of 0.56 (95% CI 0.54-0.58) on an independent US cohort while the pooled metric on the same data sat higher [holsteen2020triggers, p. 2364]. The distinction matters clinically: a model whose pooled AUROC reflects between-patient base-rate separation is useful for triaging patient-level risk strata, but it cannot tell a given patient which of *their* days is the attack. The diary-only-forecasting literature sits in the AUROC 0.56-0.73 range under within-person evaluation [houle2017stress, p. 1041; holsteen2020triggers, p. 2364], well below the wearable-augmented work that reaches higher numbers on different inputs (Faisal 2026 reports 0.84 with EMG, HRV, and skin-temperature signals on 21,550 headache days) [faisal2026forecasting, p. 1; p. 5]. The Park 2016 SHD dataset is almost entirely unexploited for machine learning despite being open under CC BY.

This work develops and internally evaluates a next-day migraine and headache forecasting benchmark on the Park 2016 SHD cohort, structured as seven layered contributions following TRIPOD+AI [collins2024tripodAI, p. 6] and PROBAST+AI [moons2025probastAI] reporting and risk-of-bias assessment. Petrušić et al. 2024 [petrusic2024evaluation, p. 1] recently published a Journal of Headache and Pain framework of six evaluation criteria for ML-classification studies in migraine; their criteria 4 (transparent training, testing, and performance evaluation), 5 (interpretability of results with clinical relevance), and 6 (open data and code) apply directly to the present forecasting study, while their criterion 1 (subgroup homogenization by clinical phenotype) targets a classification task and is reframed for the per-day prediction setting here. The contribution is reproduction-plus-extension: Holsteen et al. 2020 reported the pooled-versus-within-person discrimination gap on an independent US cohort; we reproduce the pattern on an independent Korean cohort under a different recruitment frame, and extend it with seven methodologically distinct lines of evidence (calibration forensics, SHAP attribution, a temporal-dependence battery, a sequence-model falsification test, partial-pool personalisation, decision-curve net benefit, and leave-one-site-out geographic internal-external validation), positioned against the recent narrative review of the ML-migraine-prediction field [dumkrieger2025review, p. 1].

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Cohort and outcomes

The Park 2016 SHD cohort was recruited at Uijeongbu St. Mary's and Dongtan Sacred Heart Hospital (Sept 2014 to Jan 2015), under ICHD-3 beta episodic-migraine inclusion with or without aura (60 without aura, 2 with aura; the n = 2 aura subset was retained in all analyses given its small size, with no separate aura-stratified evaluation; 19 to 55 years, with adolescent and perimenopausal / postmenopausal strata excluded by recruitment design; 2 to 14 headache days/month; at least one year of stable headache characteristics; possession of a smartphone capable of operating the SHD diary [park2016shd, p. 3]). The diary records eighteen trigger flags plus headache severity, headache type, and the day-of-headache field; the next-day prediction target was derived as `migraine_target = migraine_today.shift(-1)` per patient. The cohort comprised 4,516 patient-days at a 7.2% pooled migraine positive rate, with inter-site rates of 8.6% (Uijeongbu) and 5.7% (Dongtan). Sociodemographic variation within the cohort was minimal (82.3% female, single ethnicity), so within-cohort fairness analysis was not evaluable; the limitation is stated explicitly. Full cohort characterisation, sheet-mapping translation pipeline, and missing-data policy live in `docs/dataset.md`.

Sample-size adequacy was assessed per cell against the modern Riley / Ensor / Martin sample-size calculation for binary clinical prediction models [martin2025samplesize, p. 2], which supersedes the classical events-per-variable rule of thumb. EPV bands were: migraine `full_features` 5.5 (52 features, 287 train positives; high-risk overfitting band), migraine `park_features` 47.8 (6 features; low risk), headache `full_features` 18.2 (marginal), and ≥ 30 on the smaller-feature headache cells (low). The migraine `full_features` cell sits below the Riley criterion for adequate calibration-slope precision (target SE ≤ 0.05 on the slope), target shrinkage ≥ 0.9, and adequate CITL precision (target SE ≤ 0.05 on the calibration-in-the-large estimate); that cell is therefore reported throughout as an a priori under-powered development cell, not a discovery of inadequacy at evaluation time.

### 2.2 Predictors

Three feature sets were defined. `full_features` (52 features) layered three- and seven-day rolling means, lag features, streaks, and variability metrics on top of the eighteen Park trigger flags. `no_rolling_features` retained only the same-day flags (no history channel). `park_features` (6 features) used the six trigger flags Park et al. selected by stepwise multiple logistic regression [park2016shd, Tab. 4, p. 8]: stress, hormonal changes, noise, alcohol, overeating, travel. Numeric features were used raw (XGBoost is scale-invariant); categorical features were one-hot; no standardisation was applied for TabPFN per its in-context-learning prescription. Full predictor definitions and the Park-feature mapping live in `docs/park_features.md`.

### 2.3 Architectures

Three architecture families were evaluated. The Addition-0 XGBoost stack (`stacked_2xgb_meta_lr`) wrapped two depth-diverse XGBoost classifiers (`max_depth=3` and `max_depth=6`) with an L1-penalised logistic-regression meta-learner; a Platt sigmoid calibrated the meta-output on a held-out calibration set. Hyperparameter-tuned variants used 500-trial Optuna random search across {`learning_rate`, `max_depth`, `subsample`, `colsample_bytree`, `gamma`, `min_child_weight`, `n_estimators`}. Five TabPFN variants were evaluated under Addition 1: v2.6, v3-default, v3-binary, Real-TabPFN-v2.5, and AutoTabPFN-v2.5 (the AutoTabPFN variant evaluated on a curated 4-leaf subset rather than the full 24-leaf grid, as a deliberate compute-vs-coverage trade-off). TabPFN was used directly without external calibration; the meta-trained prior delivers calibrated probabilities natively per the v2.5 and v3 model reports. The Addition-4 sequence baselines (window-MLP, GRU, TCN) shared the same predictor stream as the tabular models. Full architecture specifications live in `docs/xgboost.md`, `docs/tabPfn.MD`, and `docs/addition4_sequence.md`. Per-leaf final-model parameters (best-trial hyperparameter JSONs for XGBoost, `model.joblib` checkpoints for TabPFN variants, full Optuna trajectory JSONL) are persisted under `experiment/0/<target>/<feature_set>/HyperparameterTuned/<strategy>/<variant>/results/best_params.json` and the equivalent paths under `experiment/1/` for TabPFN leaves, satisfying TRIPOD+AI Item 22 (full model specification enabling third-party evaluation and implementation).

### 2.4 Validation design

Three split types were evaluated: chronological (per-patient temporal hold-out, the headline regime), stratified (random with class proportions preserved), and patient hold-out (whole patients held out). Internal validation on the chronological cells used scikit-learn `TimeSeriesSplit` (expanding-window CV) on 70/15/15 splits; the cross-architecture comparison ran on 70/30 splits with the calibration sub-split derived by `chronological_subsplit(train, cal_ratio=0.20)`. External validation was geographic internal-external (leave-one-site-out across the two participating clinics of the same study): each architecture was trained on one site (Uijeongbu or Dongtan) and applied to the held-out site; operating thresholds were selected on a chronological cal sub-split of the training site only. Within-person evaluation followed the precision-weighted C-statistic of Hanley and McNeil 1982 as the per-patient discrimination estimator. Per-patient C-statistics were pooled to the cohort level by a DerSimonian-Laird random-effects model; at the small estimable-patient count for the migraine target (k = 19), DerSimonian-Laird underestimates between-patient heterogeneity τ², so the reported CI is best read as a lower bound on between-patient uncertainty. REML or Paule-Mandel τ² estimation is preferred under k < 20; the present analysis retains DerSimonian-Laird for comparability with the migraine-forecasting literature, with the lower-bound caveat above.

### 2.5 Imbalance and calibration

Class imbalance was handled by class-weighting (XGBoost `scale_pos_weight ≈ 13`, consistent with the 7.2% positive rate) followed by Platt recalibration on a held-out set, because class reweighting alone systematically distorts calibration-in-the-large [vandengoorbergh2022imbalance]; the calibration column reported in §3.4 monitors whether Platt restores it. MCC-optimal operating thresholds were selected on a held-out calibration set per cell. TabPFN's posterior estimation was left untouched, consistent with the v2.5 and v3 model-report claim of calibrated probabilities from the meta-trained prior; whether this property holds empirically on this cohort is reported in the ECE10 and Brier columns. Calibration was measured with intercept, slope, ECE10, and Brier score per Huang et al. 2020 [huang2020calibration, p. 621].

### 2.6 Explainability, temporal characterisation, sequence-model test

Addition 2 layered SHAP (KernelSHAP on the calibrated XGBoost probability; TabPFN-native explainer on TabPFN leaves), ALE, and order-2 ShapIQ k-SII interactions on the headline cells; interaction analysis was confined to the EPV-appropriate migraine `park_features` cell (EPV 47.8), with the EPV-5.5 migraine `full_features` cell excluded a priori per the sample-size constraint stated in §2.1. SHAP attribution on observational diary data quantifies association rather than cause; influential predictors identified by it are reported here as ranked associations, not as established attack-causing exposures. Addition 3 ran a calendar-regular gap-aware temporal-dependence battery on the daily attack indicator (pooled and per-patient ACF with Ljung-Box tests, first-order Markov, discrete-time self-excitation logistic regression controlling for same-day triggers, Goh-Barabasi burstiness, Andersen-Gill and Prentice-Williams-Peterson recurrent-event regression, day-of-week omnibus). Addition 4 fitted the sequence baselines on the same feature stream as a falsification test of the IID-row assumption.

### 2.7 Personalisation and clinical value

Addition 5 evaluated three personalisation regimes sharing one logistic-regression base: pooled, per-patient, and partial-pool (empirical-Bayes per-patient random intercept). The within-person C-statistic and the per-patient AUROC distribution were estimated by out-of-fold cross-validation on the non-hyperparameter-tuned 70/30 chronological leaves. Addition 6 layered decision-curve net benefit [vickers2006dca; vickers2019dca] and a Brier skill score against each patient's TRAIN-set climatology [murphy1993forecast]. The decision-curve threshold range was pre-specified at 0.01-0.50 (50-point linspace); the clinically plausible band for pre-emptive abortive medication sits at the low end, since a missed attack carries day-level disability cost while an unnecessary dose carries low cost plus a modest medication-overuse risk, favouring low operating thresholds. No new core models were fitted; the value layer scored saved test predictions.

### 2.8 Statistical comparison and reporting discipline

Every metric was reported as `mean [95% CI]` from 1,000-iteration bootstrap with patient-day as the resampling unit. Within-patient serial dependence (Addition 3) is therefore not propagated into the bootstrap CI; the patient-day-iid CI is conservative against an architectural-equivalence null and may under-cover relative to a patient-cluster bootstrap. Cross-architecture comparisons used paired DeLong tests (Sun-Xu midrank implementation) with Benjamini-Hochberg false-discovery-rate correction across 18 pre-specified contrasts and Bonferroni FWER correction on the full all-pairs cross-cell family for sensitivity. Paired DeLong assumes within-class independence; on patient-day data the assumption is not exact, but Type-I error inflation under the violation runs toward more significance, so the 0-of-166-pair null at the canonical headline cells is conservative against the assumption. Tie handling on post-Platt calibrated outputs used midrank averaging (Sun-Xu 2014). The TRIPOD+AI 27-item compliance trail per section is provided in supplementary material; the per-item map lives in `docs/paper_rigor_checklist.md`.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Cohort flow and base rates

Sixty-two enrolled patients yielded 63 unique processed `patient_id` values after diary deduplication and 4,516 patient-days at 7.2% pooled migraine positive rate (Uijeongbu 8.6% vs Dongtan 5.7%); cohort-flow detail in Figure A2. Forty-nine patients had estimable per-patient lag-1 autocorrelation for the migraine target; 62 patients had estimable autocorrelation for the headache target.

### 3.2 Headline discrimination per Addition

On chronological splits the headline migraine AUROC reached 0.793 (95% CI 0.701-0.873; XGB-HP020 stack, `full_features` 70/30) and the headline headache AUROC reached 0.652 (95% CI 0.589-0.712; TabPFN family at `full / chrono / 70/30`: v2.6, v3-default, and v3-binary tied within the composite rule's 0.02 AUROC tier, v2.6 picked by calibration-distance tiebreak). The 166-pair Bonferroni family was the all-pairs sensitivity escalation of the 18 pre-specified BH-FDR contrasts named in §2.8: 0 of 166 pairs were significant at the canonical headline cells, so the family-level (within-architecture-family tie at the headache cell) result is the defensible cross-architecture claim. Detailed per-cell tables are in `docs/results_findings.md`.

### 3.3 Stratified-split optimism is feature-channel leakage

Stratified splits inflated AUROC only on feature sets with history features (`full_features`, `spano_features`). Feature sets without history (`no_rolling_features`, `park_features`) showed no inflation; stratified was in fact slightly worse than chronological for those cells. SHAP attribution corroborated the leakage channel by mean absolute |SHAP| (sign-invariant, so unaffected by the Platt-inversion cells in §3.4): the history features (`migraine_rate_last7`, `headache_free_streak`, `days_since_last_migraine`) carried 77 to 85% of mean absolute attribution on `full_features` chronological cells and gained both rank and magnitude under stratification (e.g. `migraine_rate_last7` rose from rank 3 to rank 1 on the headache stratified cell, a 2.4-fold magnitude shift). The chronological split is the methodologically honest evaluation; stratified is reported only to quantify the leakage channel.

### 3.4 Calibration is fragile on the small or sparse cells

Median cross-cell calibration slope was 0.64 (over-confident); on the headline cells specifically the migraine slope was 1.417 (95% CI 0.952-1.942) and the headache slope 1.095 (95% CI 0.744-1.427), both CIs including 1.0 (though the migraine point estimate sits materially above unity, so the CI-containing-1.0 reading is necessary but not sufficient evidence of well-calibrated slope on that cell). Calibration-in-the-large (CITL = mean(y) / mean(p), with 1.0 indicating no systematic bias) was 1.09 [0.79, 1.42] for migraine XGBoost, 1.14 [0.83, 1.48] for migraine TabPFN, 1.03 [0.88, 1.18] for headache XGBoost, and 0.98 [0.84, 1.12] for headache TabPFN; all four headline CITL CIs contained 1.0, so the headline cells were not systematically over- or under-forecasting at the cohort level. Among comparator architectures on the migraine `full_features / 70_30 / chrono` cell, AutoTabPFN (`version_2-5-auto`) reached calibration slope 0.992 [0.642, 1.346], closer to 1.0 than both the migraine XGB-HP020 headline slope of 1.417 above and the migraine TabPFN-v2-5-finetuned slope of 0.964 [0.604, 1.346]; all three migraine slope CIs included 1.0, so the architectures did not separate at the CI level despite the point-estimate ordering. AutoTabPFN AUROC 0.745 [0.656, 0.825] sat within the XGB-HP020 AUROC CI [0.701, 0.873]. Across the 488-cell grid, 98 cells had a negative slope, the Platt-inversion fingerprint (the calibrator inverts when hyperparameter search overfits a leaky split and the fitted sigmoid takes a negative slope) of small-calibration-set overfitting. The pattern concentrated in HP-tuned XGBoost on stratified splits (where the inner CV's calibration sub-split is statistically thin) and on the sparse migraine patient-hold-out cells. TabPFN's single-forward-pass in-context inference did not exhibit this collapse: of 42 anti-predictive cells (hold-out AUROC < 0.45) across the 488-cell grid, 38 were `stacked_2xgb_meta_lr` and only four were TabPFN, all marginal. The calibration framing is the methodological reason this study reports both discrimination and calibration; reporting AUC alone hides the cells where the meta-learner's posterior collapses.

### 3.5 Temporal dependence justifies a sequence-model test but not its dominance

The daily attack series carried strong short-range within-patient serial dependence in both targets, confirmed by four independent methods. The pooled lag-1 autocorrelation was 0.267 for migraine and 0.218 for headache; the trigger-controlled discrete-time self-excitation logistic regression gave a lag-1 odds ratio of 5.43 for migraine (LR vs triggers-only χ² = 155, p = 2.5e-33) and 2.44 for headache (χ² = 203, p = 1.1e-43); the first-order Markov transition P(attack tomorrow | attack today) was 0.315 for migraine versus 0.050 without (risk ratio 6.3). Goh-Barabasi burstiness B clustered near zero (median -0.05 migraine, -0.06 headache), so the dependence was short-range day-to-day clustering rather than long-tailed bursty memory [houle2005timeseries, p. 445; barra2020markov, p. 3; goh2008burstiness, p. 2]. Addition 4's sequence baselines (window-MLP, GRU, TCN) did not unseat the XGBoost-stack headline on the chronological full_features cells. The verdict was "competitive but not dominant" rather than a uniform null: the short-range temporal-signal prediction of Addition 3 was supported, but the engineered lag and rolling features in the tabular models already encoded the recoverable signal. Cross-architecture differences at the val+test holdout horizon (n = 575 patient-days) were not statistically separable: every architecture's bootstrap 95% AUROC CI contained the other four architectures' point estimates in both targets (Figure B7), so the verdict held on the CI structure as well as on the serial-dependence theory.

### 3.6 Within-person evaluation: the load-bearing measurement

Under out-of-fold cross-validation on the non-hyperparameter-tuned 70/30 chronological leaves (HP-tuned leaves were excluded from the within-person measurement because their calibrated probabilities carry the Platt-inversion fragility documented in §3.4), the within-person C-statistic clustered at 0.53 to 0.57 for every architecture and both targets, near chance and architecture-independent (TabPFN headache 0.542 [0.508-0.576] on 57 of 63 estimable patients; TabPFN migraine 0.563 [0.500-0.626] on 19 of 63 estimable patients at the principled five-positive floor). The pooled-minus-within gap reached 0.175 AUROC for migraine TabPFN (pooled 0.738 → within-person 0.563), the largest gap in the sweep. The within-person architecture differences sat inside overlapping CIs, statistically indistinguishable per patient. The result reproduced the within-person Holsteen 2020 pattern on an independent cohort [holsteen2020triggers, p. 2364].

### 3.7 Clinical value: forecast quality versus forecast value

Brier skill against per-patient climatology was significantly positive for headache across all architectures (XGBoost +0.198 [+0.126, +0.268]; TabPFN +0.215 [+0.143, +0.287]; sequence +0.139 [+0.066, +0.211]) and at most marginally negative for migraine: only the sequence baseline carried a CI fully below zero (-0.117 [-0.227, -0.037]); the XGBoost migraine CI included zero (-0.068 [-0.160, +0.005]) and the TabPFN migraine CI included zero (-0.057 [-0.146, +0.020]) despite migraine AUROC ~0.76. High migraine discrimination therefore did not translate into significant probabilistic value beyond the per-patient base rate forecast, but the migraine tabular-architecture skill was statistically indistinguishable from the climatology baseline rather than significantly worse — exactly Murphy's quality-versus-value distinction with finite-sample uncertainty acknowledged [murphy1993forecast, p. 281]. Decision-curve net benefit (Figure D3) on the migraine target sat near zero across the pre-specified threshold band (0.01-0.50), with patient-day bootstrap 95% CI bands that included zero at every threshold for all three architectures, whereas the headache architectures dominated both the treat-all and treat-none reference policies across the same band (net benefit +0.05 to +0.20, with bootstrap CI bands non-overlapping with zero throughout).

### 3.8 Geographic internal-external validation: calibration drift tracks the inter-site base-rate gap

Leave-one-site-out validation across Uijeongbu (8.6% migraine) and Dongtan (5.7%) reproduced the within-person near-chance result off-site (within-person C 0.46 to 0.59 across architectures, one cell below 0.50). Pooled discrimination held across sites (migraine AUROC 0.62 to 0.80 on the held-out site). Calibration drifted with the site base rate: a model carried from the 8.6% site to the 5.7% site over-predicted (O:E fell to 0.50; on the maximally-drifting window-MLP cell the held-out 2-parameter Platt refit returned intercept α = -1.52 [-1.82, -1.22] and slope 0.55 [0.42, 0.71]); carried the other way it under-predicted (O:E rose to 1.54; on the maximally-drifting XGBoost-stack cell the refit returned α = +2.41 [+1.35, +3.62] and slope 0.37 [0.26, 0.49]). The reported (α, β) come from the joint 2-parameter Platt refit on the held-out site; by construction the joint refit recovers both mean and slope calibration on the test data, the joint α encodes the logit-scale shift the transport miscalibration demanded under simultaneous slope correction, and the below-unity slope values show that even after intercept adjustment the predictions remain over-confident, with the joint refit absorbing the residual over-confidence into the slope term. An alternative intercept-only Platt refit (β fixed at 1) would also recover mean calibration on the held-out site, with a smaller |α| but no slope correction. The TabPFN family was the calibration-robust outlier: |joint α| sat near zero in both transport directions (Uijeongbu→Dongtan: α = +0.26 [-0.19, +0.72], slope 1.14 [0.96, 1.34]; Dongtan→Uijeongbu: α = -0.16 [-0.57, +0.26], slope 0.76 [0.61, 0.93]), consistent with the meta-trained prior delivering calibrated probabilities under mild cohort shift. The mean predicted risk tracked the training site, not the test site. No in-study model updating or recalibration was performed.

## 4. Discussion

### 4.1 The pooled-AUROC framing overstates within-person value on this cohort

The benchmark's load-bearing finding is that the pooled AUROC commonly reported in the next-day-diary migraine-forecasting literature overstates within-person forecasting skill on the Park 2016 SHD cohort. The chronological headline cell delivers migraine AUROC 0.793 (95% CI 0.701-0.873) and headache AUROC 0.652 (95% CI 0.589-0.712), yet the within-person C-statistic clusters near 0.55 (TabPFN headache 0.542 [0.508-0.576]; TabPFN migraine 0.563 [0.500-0.626]) and the per-patient AUROC distribution centres near chance. Pooled discrimination on this cohort is therefore overwhelmingly between-patient base-rate separation, not within-person day-to-day ranking. The implication for clinical deployment is direct: at the present cohort size and diary-only input regime, the per-patient day-to-day ranking does not support an individual-patient pre-emptive medication decision. Within-person C clusters at chance and the per-patient AUROC distribution centres on 0.5, so a forecast that beats chance pooled does not let a given patient act on *their* day-by-day predictions. This finding reproduces the within-person Holsteen 2020 pattern on an independent cohort and clarifies why the diary-only forecasting regime sits in the AUROC 0.56 to 0.73 band that the field reports under within-person evaluation, well below the wearable-augmented high-water mark.

### 4.2 Each Addition supplies an independent line of evidence

Six Additions converge on the pooled-overstates conclusion through different methodological lenses, so the finding does not rest on any single measurement. Addition 0 establishes the calibration fragility of HP-tuned XGBoost stacking on small calibration sets. Addition 1 shows that the TabPFN family matches or slightly exceeds XGBoost on the headache headline cell without per-leaf hyperparameter tuning and is more robust than HP-tuned XGBoost on small or leakage-prone splits. Addition 2 layers SHAP attribution on the calibrated probability and shows that the history features carry 77 to 85% of mean absolute attribution on `full_features` cells while contributing 0% on `no_rolling`, corroborating the stratified-split leakage mechanism by an independent method. Addition 3 quantifies the temporal dependence directly and predicts that any sequence-model gain will be small because the recoverable signal sits in a short window the engineered lag and rolling features already encode. Addition 4 confirms that prediction: no sequence variant beats the XGBoost stack headline. Addition 5 supplies the load-bearing within-person measurement and shows that partial pooling's pooled-AUROC gain (+0.10 to +0.13 over pooled) does not survive the within-person reframing.

### 4.3 Field implications

Three implications for the diary-only migraine-forecasting subfield follow. First, calibration slope, decision-curve net benefit, and a within-person C-statistic should be reported alongside pooled discrimination, so that papers can be compared on the metric that matches the clinical use case rather than on pooled AUROC alone. Second, AUROC alone is insufficient when the clinical action is a probability-thresholded pre-emptive medication decision: the migraine target reaches AUROC ~0.76 yet returns negative Brier skill against each patient's own climatology. Third, the largest open trigger-diary cohort in the public record, the Park 2016 SHD dataset, is almost entirely unexploited for machine learning; this work provides published reference points for the diary-only forecasting regime that prior wearable-augmented work cannot supply.

### 4.4 Strengths and limitations

Strengths: pre-specified evaluation regime, calibration reported alongside discrimination on every cell, within-person C-statistic and decision-curve net benefit on the canonical leaves, leave-one-site-out validation across the two recruitment clinics, open code and data. Limitations: single-cohort study, n = 62 with effectively no within-cohort sociodemographic variation; no separate-cohort external validation (the Park-group 82-patient SHED extension was never deposited publicly); the chronological split is patient-overlapping by design; the EPV-5.5 migraine `full_features` cell sits in the high-risk overfitting band; PROBAST+AI applicability is narrow (geographic internal-external validation across two clinics of one study, not separate-cohort transport). Fairness across underrepresented groups is not testable within this cohort. Phenotype-mixing sensitivity (per criterion 1 of [petrusic2024evaluation, p. 1]): of the n = 2 patients with migraine with aura, one (DHA-0057, 20 of 575 chronological-split test rows) fell into the headline test split and the other (CMC-0026) was train-side only; excluding DHA-0057's test rows shifts headline migraine AUROC from 0.791 to 0.704 (Δ = -0.087) and headache AUROC from 0.653 to 0.611 (Δ = -0.042), both within the reported 95% CIs but the migraine shift sits at the lower-bound edge. Calibration slope on the no-aura subset sits closer to unity (migraine 0.92, headache 0.91 versus 1.42, 1.09 on the full cohort), so the aura patient's predictions contributed to the full-cohort over-confidence pattern. The cohort-level conclusions — pooled-AUROC-overstates-within-person, headache-positive-Brier-skill, migraine-null-or-negative-Brier-skill — survive the exclusion; the substantive shift in pooled AUROC under a 3.5%-of-rows exclusion is itself further evidence for the body §3.6 finding that pooled discrimination on this cohort is largely between-patient base-rate separation rather than within-patient day-to-day ranking.

### 4.5 Future work

Three explicit next directions: (i) true out-of-cohort external validation on the 82-patient SHED extension [cho2018exercise; park2018circadian], gated on a data-sharing request to the original authors; (ii) wearable augmentation that brings the diary-only AUROC into the Faisal 2026 wearable-augmented band; (iii) multi-cohort deep personalisation, the natural sequel once a second cohort is in hand. The present benchmark does not evaluate deployment-stage usability; the relevant reporting guideline for that stage is DECIDE-AI [vasey2022decideAI].

## 5. Conclusion

We benchmarked 24-hour migraine and headache forecasting on the Park 2016 SHD cohort across seven layered contributions and reported bootstrap discrimination CIs, calibration slope, decision-curve net benefit, and within-person evaluation together. Under within-person evaluation the per-patient AUROC distribution centred near 0.55, and the precision-weighted within-person C-statistic clustered in the same band (0.53 to 0.57 across architectures and both targets, estimable on 57 of 63 patients for headache and 19 of 63 for migraine). The chronological pooled headline cell reached migraine AUROC 0.793 (95% CI 0.701-0.873) and headache AUROC 0.652 (95% CI 0.589-0.712); pooled discrimination commonly reported in the next-day-diary migraine-forecasting literature therefore overstates within-person forecasting skill on this cohort, reflecting between-patient base-rate separation more than within-patient day-to-day ranking. Headache forecasts retained probabilistic value across the clinically plausible threshold band (Brier skill positive for every architecture). The migraine target, in contrast, returned negative Brier skill despite AUROC ~0.76: AUROC ranking did not beat the trivial per-patient base-rate forecast on a proper scoring rule. Future diary-only migraine-forecasting work in this subfield will need to report calibration slope, decision-curve net benefit, and a within-person C-statistic alongside pooled discrimination, so that papers can be compared on the metric that matches the clinical use case rather than on pooled AUROC alone.
